# BEARERS — Design Rules
*Internal. Not built to PDF. Constraints for writing cards, Callings, monsters and spells, the reasons behind the rules, and fifth-edition conversion. "(P 6.5)" points to the Player Rules, "(GM 3)" to the Game Master's Guide.*

---

## 1. Pillars
Three rules pillars, **in priority order**. When two conflict, the higher one wins.

1. **Snappy and simple.** Simpler than D&D: few rules, few numbers to track, fast turns. A rule earns its place only if it changes decisions at the table; anything done every turn or every attack (a count, a lookup, a token) must stay a glance.
2. **Strategic.** Bad decisions get punished. Positioning, range, target priority, hit-and-run and surrounding carry real weight. A tactical rule is added only if it stays simple: one count, one number, no exceptions to memorize.
3. **Everything stacks.** Every bonus, penalty and die adds up, with as few exceptions as possible. An exception is allowed only when stacking would slow play (pillar 1) or flatten choices (pillar 2), and each one is listed below with its reason.

Also: interactive · lots of character customization · balanced. Power comes from a fixed Calling + Runes + gear; characters have no Rank or level, and all growth is cards (and Slots).

**Stacking exceptions** (the full list; add any new one here):
- A condition doesn't stack with itself, and a power doesn't stack with itself (P 4.6, 8): repeat casts would otherwise need per-instance tracking (1).
- No two copies of the same card (P 4.6): one card, one effect, keeps Slots a choice (2).
- Situations give one die per question (You, Target, Crowd), however many reasons each has (P 6.6): three glances instead of a count (1). Combining kinds of tactics still pays, piling one kind on doesn't (2).
- Temporary HP keep the higher amount (P 7.3); two resistances to one type count as one (P 7.2): balance (Slots spent on survival would make a Bearer untouchable).
- A roll is rerolled only once (P 11.4): stops chains of Cues (1).
- Area rolls ignore each target's situations and Outnumbered (P 6.6): one roll for everyone (1), and clustering has a cost (2).
- Subdue repeats ask no questions (P 8): only decay dice, one count beside the target (1).
- Outnumbered gives one die however big the difference (P 6.5): the Crowd question (1).
- A Form ignores gear (P 9.6): it's a second body, not a stack on the first.

## 1b. Lore Constraints
- Player characters are **Bearers** (P 2.1). Powers are **Runes**: signs that magical creatures nearly always carry for life (other creatures only by exception: a veteran, a master), and that a Bearer can **etch** and take out again. Everything else is **gear**, a real object. Each Bearer has a **Calling** (the pair of abilities their past has shaped: what they have lived, not what they are), which is never etched and never lost; only a Rank V Rune rewrites it. A Bearer's **body and personality never change**, except that a Form (P 9.6) changes the shape for a while. A shifted Bearer always reverts and keeps their mind.
- Write a Rune as a power, a tool with no past; a Calling as what someone has lived; and the other types as objects someone could pick up.
- **Naming rule for Runes.** A Rune names a power, never a person, a trade, a race or a memory, and the name must read naturally after "Rune of" (*Iron Will*, *Dusk Eyes*, *Forge*, *Navigation*; not *Elven Blood*, *Smith*, *Sailor*). A Domain is named after its domain: one noun that covers its four activities (*Navigation*, *Backstreets*, *Larceny*). Two crafts of performance and lore keep a trade name on purpose, *Minstrel* and *Herbalist*: the word names the art better than any noun. Titles stay short in the rules (*Tavern*); the card prints "Rune of" in small above the name.
- **Why Runes (2026-11).** Playtesters found the first fiction creepy: Bearers absorbed the Aspects of defeated beings, which read as stealing memories and identities. The mechanic was never in question, so only the fiction changed. The intent is the fantasy version of taking a beaten machine's program: a Rune is a removable tool, not a piece of someone, which also justifies rebuilding at every rest (you grow by what you find, not by what you lived). What follows from it:
  - **Species has no card**, and no Rune tells a life: the old species and profession cards kept their numbers under power names. Trade names were tried and dropped (2026-11-11): a trade still reads as a person.
  - **A Rune comes loose at death, or is given.** A living creature that isn't a Bearer gives a **copy** and keeps its own, so talk and mercy stay rewarded and nothing is taken from the living. A Bearer never copies, so a party can't duplicate its best Rune.
  - **Anchoring: one cause, three answers (P 2.1; GM 6.3).** In any other creature a Rune is *anchored*; in a Bearer it never anchors. That is why a Bearer can take a Rune out, why only a non-Bearer's Rune can be copied, and why a Rune taken out leaves no skill behind (the Rune did the work, not the hands). Death frees a Rune from its anchor, so a captured Rune can't be copied either. The sign is only the Rune's trace, so nobody copies a Rune by drawing it. *Anchored* was preferred to *rooted*: too organic, too close to the first unease. It is a plain word, not a game term: no capital, no glossary entry.
  - **Who carries Runes (2026-11-13).** Mostly magical creatures; an ordinary human (a guard, a knight) seldom does, and grows through gear. Foe cards keep their Rune drops as what such a foe *may* carry, and the loot share between gear and Runes is unchanged: Runes also come from gifts, quests and magical foes. Rare humans who carry one (a master, a priest, a noble) can still give a copy.
  - **Only Rank IV and V Runes have a temperament** and echoes (GM 6.3); lower Runes are mute tools.
  - **Left to the GM (GM 6):** where Runes come from, whether etched Runes show on the body, and why some regions hunt Bearers. The rules give no motive.
  - **Old terms:** Aspect → Rune, Shard → Runestone, Absorb → Etch, Knack → Domain, Remembrance → Instinct. Capture, Lingering and Release kept their names.

## 2. Power Ceiling
Rank IV cards, 8 Runes and all three extra Gear Slots are the **peak**, roughly a level-12 hero in fifth edition. Rank IV is slightly game-breaking; Rank V is meta and game-breaking, a Boss treasure players hunt for.

Power scaling comes mainly from Rank (better cards). The Calling never grows: Armor and Traits carry the HP growth the Core used to (section 4, Callings). Slot count grows slowly: 3 → 8 Runes, plus at most three extra Gear Slots. Five milestones (one per story; a story is 3–5 quests of 3–5 trials, about 15 trials) ≈ fifth edition to level 12.

## 3. Rank Scaling
| Rank | Bonus | Power reference | Spellbook (typical; + INT on the tally card) | Armor HP (light / medium / heavy) | Trait HP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | +2 | A small edge: a narrow check bonus, a basic weapon or armor, a minor trait | +2 points, max 2nd | +2 / +4 / +6 | +2 |
| **II** | +3 | A defining feature: an extra attack, heavy armor, a reliable power | +5 points, max 3rd | +4 / +8 / +12 | +4 |
| **III** | +4 | A strong feature: a magic weapon or armor, a signature power | +9 points, max 5th | +7 / +14 / +21 | +7 |
| **IV** | +5 | Slightly game-breaking: a legendary item, a power that bends a core rule | +14 points, max 6th | +10 / +20 / +30 | +10 |
| **V** | +6 | Game-breaking: changes the balance of play (meta Runes, rule-breaking gear, size beyond Large, a new Calling) | +18 points, max 6th, plus one rule-breaking power | +14 / +28 / +42 | +14 |

*HP columns provisional until the sim pass.* A **body** of a given Rank (a Form, a foe built from scratch) keeps the old Core sizing: modifiers total +4 / +6 / +8 / +10 (max +3 / +4 / +5 / +5) and 12 / 30 / 58 / 90 HP at Rank I–IV.

- **WIL magic is one mechanism in several flavors (P 9.5; decided 2026-11-12).** Every WIL deck has the same structure: a cover that prints Attack, Resolve and one flavor line, the same Resolve pool, and the same voice rule (every power but a Form). The type (Divine, Glamour, Nature) and the deck's name (Litany, Repertoire, Instinct) are flavor: the **only** thing a type decides is which powers its deck holds (Litany and Repertoire: Anthems, Dirges, Cues, Mends; Instinct: Forms, Mends). Each flavor keeps its own powers together: a free mix (a Form in a Litany) was considered and dropped the same day, since the content is what makes a flavor. So a new source of WIL magic (a pact, a totem, an oath) is a new flavor with its own content, never a new subsystem, and no card may say "your Divine powers". Before this, Nature needed no voice and printed no Attack, Divine printed its fate die on the cover, and a Glamour dance could be seen instead of heard: the first two exceptions are gone, and the third became the **Visual** keyword. **Conditions belong to each power, never to a type**: a power that departs from the default (heard) prints it, like its range or its target. No base set power is Visual yet. Resolve = printed + WIL (minimum 1); printed by Rank: I 3 · II 4 · III 5 · IV 6 · V 7, back on a short rest. Anthems, Cues and Forms don't roll, so anyone can splash them; WIL buys **more uses**, not more power per use. Powers cost 0–3. Anthem and Dirge bonuses and penalties equal the Rank bonus; Rank III+ powers may grant an advantage or disadvantage die. Each flavor has one identity, which its content carries: **Glamour** gives flat bonuses and penalties (the Rank bonus); **Divine** alters fate with rerolls and fate dice (d4 at I–II, d6 at III, d8 at IV–V) and heals; **Nature** shifts and heals. Divine and Nature heal most; a Repertoire carries one Mend at most (*Rousing Verse*), so a Glamour starter can patch an ally up without becoming the healer. Watch healing: a Templar or Performer (WIL +2) with a Rank I Litany has 5 Resolve, three Mends per short rest. **Mends** cost at least 2, since Resolve comes back on a short rest and a short rest already heals half your missing HP; heal by Rank like a 5e spell of the deck's max level. Anthems stack like every bonus: keep them narrow (one roll type) and watch two-performer parties in playtests. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **Rank II WIL decks (loot, 2026-11-13):** one per type, each with new powers rather than bigger copies of the Rank I ones (except the at-will Dirge and Cue a flavor needs): *Herald's Repertoire*, *Vicar's Litany*, *Prowler's Instinct*, *Supplicant's Pact*. Attack WIL + 3, Resolve +4, bonuses and penalties 3, fate die d4, Mends 3d4 / 3d8 for one creature (cost 2) or 2d4 / 2d6 for up to 3 allies (cost 3). Forms are sized like a Rank II body (modifiers total +6, max +4; HP 10 / 20 / 30 by cost; brute hide +3). Glamour's *Rout* is the first area Dirge for Bearers (each enemy within 30 ft, Frightened until the end of your next turn, like the Chieftain's *Bellow*). *Malediction* (−3 to all Defenses for 1 minute, cost 1) is the strongest debuff per Resolve: watch it with two attackers on one target. Every Pact power targets WIL Defense, an off-Defense on most foes (Ogre 10), so *Hex Bolt* hits more often than *Fire Bolt* (DEX): watch Pact damage against Rank II foes in the sim. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **Forms (P 9.6)** are bodies, printed like a Calling's strip. Cost 1 (utility: scouting, flying, swimming), 2 (fighting) or 3 (brutes). STR and DEX are sized like a body of the Instinct's Rank (section 3: Rank I total about +4, max +3). HP pool by cost: ⅓, ⅔ or all of that Rank's body HP (Rank I: about 4 / 8 / 12). Brute and armored beasts may print a natural Defense bonus equal to the Rank bonus, the one Defense bonus a body ever prints, since gear is off. Natural weapons use the Form's own STR or DEX + the Rank bonus, with Rank-scaled dice: the body supplies the strength, so a WIL druid needs no STR, and WIL buys more shifts. A shift is a **fresh tally card** (Form body + Calling's INT and WIL + Runes): only Runes carry over, so a Form is a second build, and losing gear, Hands and voice is the brake on multiclassing. The separate HP pool is the second-body fantasy, and reaching 0 is the only way to lose a Form against your will (no condition ends it). The 1-hour cap keeps scouting Forms from replacing the body. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **The Rank bonus is hidden.** Players never see the term: cards print their numbers directly (weapon *+3 to hit*, book *Spell attack: INT + 3*, Domain *+3*, *Noisy 3*). Use the table's Bonus column to size every printed bonus or penalty.
- **Modifier boosts print twice.** A card that raises an ability modifier also prints the same number in the matching Defense row of its strip (+1 STR and +1 STR Defense), because Defense = 10 + modifier + bonuses. Every strip row then sums on its own.
- **Damage dice scale with Rank.** Cantrips and other growing effects print dice already scaled for their card's Rank:

| Card Rank | I–II | III | IV–V |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Damage dice** | ×1 | ×2 | ×3 |

  *Fire Bolt (1d10) in a Rank III book prints 2d10.*
- **Cards never grant dice.** Advantage and disadvantage come only from the rules: the three questions (P 6.6) and Subdue's decay (P 8). A card gives its Rank bonus as a flat bonus or penalty, or **changes a situation**: it creates one (a hit knocks Prone or Restrains, a step makes you unseen), changes a question's answer (*you count as two creatures for Crowd*, *long range gives you no disadvantage*, *Target: also when it's Frightened*) or protects (*Blur: attackers can't see you*). Dice then always come from the board, so a build never replaces positioning: Slots buy numbers, tactics buy dice. Rank IV–V cards bend a question; that is their game-breaking edge. Monsters and defensive cards answer the same way (a protective card that makes attackers unable to see you), so situations pull both ways.
- **Foe Defenses keep pace with Rank.** Foes are built from the same cards, so their Defenses grow with Rank like the Bearers' attacks (about +5 at Rank I to +12–13 at the peak). Aim a fair foe's main Defense at the party's usual attack bonus + 9 to 11 (50–60% base hit chance), where a die is worth most (+21 to +25 points between 30% and 70%). Off-Defenses don't grow (only the Calling's two +2s, Rune modifiers and armor push Defenses up): from Rank III, give a foe no Defense below 10 + its Rank bonus. *Provisional: check when Rank III cards exist.*
- **Check bonuses** cover a **narrow activity** (sneaking, climbing, lying), never a whole ability, and equal the card's Rank bonus (*Larceny*, Rank I: +2).
- **Light, precise weapons** (dagger, short blade, finesse blade, whip) deal one die size less than their heavier equivalents; their affinities lean DEX instead. Light blades keep the Rank's to-hit (+2) and get **Flurry** (+2 on later attacks with the same blade on your turn) as their edge.
- **Weapon keywords sized by expected damage** (+2 ability, Defense 13, `tools/balance_report.py`): *Greatsword* 2d6 5.75 per attack; *Greataxe* 1d12 5.42, with **Brutal** (crit: dice three times) 5.75; *Shortsword* 1d6 3.47 on the first attack, 4.02 with Flurry; *Dagger* 2.82 / 3.27. **Crushing** (*Greatclub*: Prone on a hit by 5 or more, about 1 hit in 3 against a fair Defense) is its edge over the *Glaive*'s reach; the Ogres carry it and are deliberately harder for it.
- **Every weapon has its own edge and its own cost; weapons of one Rank stay comparable (Ced, 2026-11-13).** No weapon may beat another of its Rank on every count, loot included: loot changes the trade-off, not the totals. Rank I: *Longsword* (STR, 1d8 / 1d10, **+1 STR Defense while held**) against *Battleaxe* (same dice, rolls STR or DEX); the Versatile two-handed die stays below the Heavy weapons, which pay for their dice with both Hands and the STR affinity alone. *Shortsword* (Light, Flurry) against *Gladius* (Close Order, **not Light**: the shield-line sword, no two-weapon fighting). *Longbow* (1d8, 150/600) against *Shortbow* (1d6, 80/320, **Close Shot**: an enemy within 5 ft gives no disadvantage) and *Fine Bow* (1d10, **Loading**). *Spellguard Staff* is the one weapon below the Rank's to-hit (+0, 1d6): its edge is +2 to spell and WIL deck attacks, a deliberate exception to *size every number by Rank*.
- **Magic armor, shields and weapons** are just higher-Rank cards with their own name with a bigger bonus and often a special effect (*Dragonscale*, Rank III, +5 STR Defense).

## 4. Designing Cards and Callings
**Callings and affinities** (replaced Cores; see CHANGELOG; the term *Symbols* became *Affinities / Affinités* on 2026-11-14, so the word symbol now means only the drawing):
- **Card-text nomenclature (Ced, 2026-11-14):** an **effect / effet** is passive (works on its own, no action, reaction or use); a **power / abilité** is active (an action, a reaction or a use). FR keeps *pouvoir* for WIL magic powers only; EN *power* covers both, since *ability* already means STR/DEX/INT/WIL. FR names WIL magic **le Pouvoir**, one of its cards **un pouvoir** and the deck **une Rune de Pouvoirs** (Ced, 2026-11-17; was *magie de Volonté* and *paquet de Volonté*): the deck is a Rune (P 9.5), and *pouvoir* is never used in a broad sense (a Rune is *une force en forme de signe*, a monster has *abilités*). EN keeps *WIL magic*, *WIL deck*, *power*. A Calling's perk is now called its effects and powers in the rulebooks (FR *atout* dropped there); *perk / atout* stays only as the Trait-budget unit (GM 4.3–4.5) and as shorthand in this file.
- **Twelve Callings**, two per pair of abilities: +2 / +2, +0 elsewhere (total +4, like the old starting Cores), Medium, HP 9–14 and Speed 30–35, one perk sized like a Rank I card. **Perks scale with the Calling's two abilities** (P 2.4): uses, dice and distances are ½ an ability, bonuses the whole modifier, so a perk matches its old flat value at +2 / +2 and doubles around +4 / +5 as gear raises them. Each ability drives six perks: every perk uses both of its Calling's abilities, or the Calling gets two smaller perks, one per ability (Duelist, Brawler, Spellblade). **Check perks are the one place a check adds two abilities** (Unseen, Presence, Weak Spot, Omen): a narrow edge that grows, instead of a flat +2 that Traits outgrow by Rank II. **The Trickster's *Sure Thing* keeps one flat part, on purpose** (Ced, 2026-11-12): a d20 under 10 counts as 10 on four thief checks; a floor has nothing to scale, and it grows by itself as bonuses do. Its combat part scales: +½ DEX or ½ WIL d6 (the higher) when it hits a creature that can't see it, with no uses (Ced first floored those attacks too, then traded the floor for the damage: an unseen attacker already rolls with an advantage die). It shares the ground of *Righteous Fury* and *Spell Strike* (damage on a hit) with another condition; hiding gives one such hit, since Invisible from hiding ends right after the first attack, even inside an Attack action with *Twin Strike* (Ced, 2026-11-12, P 3.5); watch it in Dark or against a Blinded foe, where every hit counts. *Riposte*, *Spell Strike* and *Slip Free* are new and the first to watch in playtests. **The two INT · WIL Callings stopped feeding pools (Ced, 2026-11-13):** *Learned* (extra spell points) and *Solitude* (extra Resolve) had the same shape and only differed by the pool. The Sage now has *Weak Spot* (a spell at one creature targets its lowest Defense, +½ INT to hit) and the Hermit *Omen* (½ WIL d20 rolled after a long rest, each one replacing the d20 of a creature within 10 ft × INT); both keep their check part. **Uses tied to INT come back on a long rest** (Ced): Spellbooks refill on a long rest, so *Weak Spot* is ½ WIL/L.R., like *Omen*'s dice. *Omen* shares ground with *Luck* and *Twist Fate* (rerolls) but fixes a roll in advance instead: watch both in playtests. STR leans to HP, no STR is fragile; the two Callings of a pair never share both HP and Speed (in four pairs one has more HP, the other 5 ft more Speed: Sage and Tactician move 35 ft), and their perks pull different ways (Duelist moves, Brawler grabs; Templar guards, Patriot strikes; Spellblade casts, Tactician commands; Infiltrator sneaks, Ranger shoots; Performer charms, Trickster never fumbles; Sage reads the foe, Hermit reads fate). A Calling has no Rank and never grows. *Provisional: HP, Speed and perks are tuned in the sim.*
- **No card duplicates a Calling perk (decided 2026-11-12).** A perk is the one thing a pair of abilities owns, since any card equips with a single lit affinity. So no gear, Rune or power may give what a perk gives, even weaker, with uses or on another trigger: **the same effect is a duplicate**; sharing a perk's ground is not (*Shield Wall* beside *Oath*, *Boots of the Long Stride* beside *Footwork*, a Domain's +2 on the checks of *Unseen*). Check every new card against the twelve perks in P 13.2. Each perk draws on both of its Calling's abilities, or the Calling has two perks, one for each (this used to be player text in P 2.4; it is a design rule). Either side may yield: a card that stands alone against a perk changes or leaves; a perk whose effect is common currency in the set changes instead. First casualty: *Sidestep* (a reaction to move without provoking) doubled the Duelist's *Footwork* and left the set; the Goblin keeps the move as its own ability, *Nimble*. *Battle Hymn* gave allies +2 on their next attack after your hit and outdid the Ranger's *Quarry*: it now gives +2 to STR and DEX Defense until your next turn. **Exception:** one more point in a pool (the Focus gear's extra Resolve, *Herbalist*'s spell point) is not a power, and no Calling gives extra points since *Learned* and *Solitude* left (2026-11-13). The Trickster's *Feint* (a reaction that turns a hit into a miss: *Ring of Warding*, *Cutting Words*, the *Shield* spell) and the Performer's *Crowd Work* (+2 on an ally's roll after the fact: *Steady Now*, *Guidance*) were the common currency of the set, so the two perks changed, not the cards: the Performer has *Presence* (perform, persuade and deceive add DEX and WIL), the Trickster *Sure Thing* (Ced's own idea, after refusing reaction tricks as not interesting enough). Both DEX · WIL Callings now fight with their cards alone, like the Infiltrator: watch their survival in playtests (9 and 10 HP, no defensive perk).
- **Gating is loose on purpose:** a card is equipped if the Calling has **any one** of its lit affinities. A 2-affinity card fits 5 of 6 Callings (only the opposite pair is locked out), a 1-affinity card 3 of 6, an all-four card everyone. With the base set, every Calling can equip most of the 14 starter Hand cards (basic weapons light all four, except the heavy ones and the *Spellguard Staff*) and 13–17 of the 19 starter Runes. The identity comes from the base +2s, the perk and the **one-affinity cards** (decks, heavy gear). **The lever:** to sharpen identity, give more cards a single symbol; to loosen it, more cards all four.
- **Affinity parity (check with `python3 tools/balance_report.py`).** A pair is locked out of its opposite pair's two-affinity cards and of the single-affinity cards of its two missing abilities. Equal access therefore needs **equal two-affinity counts per pair** and **equal single-affinity counts per ability**; the report counts what each pair can equip and actually use (a Focus needs a WIL deck, a Shield Technique a Shield). Base set: 80–92 usable cards of 112 (starter 58–69), after the starter expansion of 2026-11-12 and the Rank II WIL decks. Single-affinity identity cards: STR 7 (Plate, *Boarspear*, *Greatclub*, *Greataxe*, *Greatsword*, *Glaive*, *Sweeping Blow*), WIL 13 (decks and Focus gear: *Traveler's Lute*, *Holy Amulet*, *Dancer's Bells*), INT 8 (the Spellbooks, *Wand*, *Arcane Crystal*), DEX 2 (*Shortbow*, *Fine Bow*); **heavy weapons light STR alone, starters included** (Ced, 2026-11-13). Infiltrator and Ranger (DEX · INT) still see the least loot. *Open:* more DEX and INT identity cards (a DEX Technique) rather than taking STR's away; the four new Spellbooks and *Arcane Crystal* added INT ones.
- **Every lit affinity is a roll option.** A card that rolls names abilities from its own affinities, ideally all of them (*Battleaxe*, STR · DEX: *1d8 + STR or DEX*), so whoever can equip it rolls with a +2, never +0. Weapons attack with the higher of the abilities they name; basic weapons light more affinities than they roll (below). Decks light one affinity because their roll is one ability: Spellbooks INT, Divine, Glamour and Nature WIL. A card that triggers a general rule's roll (*Shield Bash* shoves with STR) is the one known gap: give such cards an affinity pair that includes the rule's ability.
- **Assigning affinities:** **A weapon's roll is STR, DEX or both**, never INT or WIL: heavy weapons STR alone, light and thrown weapons STR · DEX, precise blades and ranged weapons DEX alone, Versatile weapons STR or STR · DEX. **Staves and wands are the one exception:** they roll INT or WIL, one die size less than the matching weapon (*Spellguard Staff*, +0 to hit, 1d6, and +2 to spell and WIL deck attacks instead: Ced, 2026-11-13, it no longer counts as an empty Hand for casting; *Wand*, INT, 1d8 Lightning at range, against the *Fine Bow*'s 1d10), so an INT or WIL Bearer has a weapon that never outclasses a STR or DEX one. **Basic (starter) weapons light all four affinities** for compatibility: anyone can wield one, at +0 if the Calling lacks its roll. Two exceptions: the *Spellguard Staff* lights only INT · WIL, so STR and DEX Callings take a real weapon instead, and **heavy weapons light STR alone, starters included** (*Greatsword*, *Glaive*, *Greatclub*, *Greataxe*; Ced, 2026-11-13). **Loot weapons light only their roll** (heavy ones STR alone, replacing the old Requirements). Heavy armor STR alone. This is the one place a card's affinities and its roll differ (see *Every lit affinity is a roll option* above). Runes: two by theme, spread so each Calling gets a fair share of each Rank. Jewelry, Consumables, Recipes, Containers and Runestones: all four (anyone). **Focus gear lights WIL alone**, whatever its Slot: only a WIL deck uses it, so lighting more would let a Calling equip a card that does nothing. Same test for any card: a lit affinity must open a Calling that can use the effect.
- **HP lives on Armor and Traits** (section 3 table). Heavier armor gives more HP and less Speed; **every Trait prints HP** by Rank (a Trait changes the body); Techniques and Domains print none. Peak check: Templar 14 + Rank IV heavy armor 30 + four Rank IV Traits 40 = 84 HP (the old Rank IV Core: 80–100). Fragile end: a Sage in light armor with two Traits, 9 + 10 + 20 = 39. Start: Templar in Mail with two Traits 22, Sage in Leather 11 (old starting Cores 9–14). *Open: is the spread too wide? Sim pass.*
- **Rank V can rewrite a Calling:** a Rank V Rune that, when etched, changes the Calling for good and is used up. Cards that no longer match stop working until swapped out. Name and card TBD (none in the base set).
- **Foes have no Calling:** they use any card. A foe's body is plain text; its HP leaves out the HP its Armor and Traits print. When Cores were removed, every base-set foe kept its exact totals (body = old Core, minus card HP), so the sim measured only the player-side change.
- **Species has no card.** A Bearer's species is description only. The six Rank I Runes that began as species cards are plain Runes any humanoid can carry (Traits *Grit*, *Dusk Eyes*, *Bedrock*, *Luck*: a body edge; Domains *Campaign*, *Discipline*: four activities and no HP, so they never beat a plain Domain): Rank I loot within the Trait budget (GM 4.3): +2 HP (Traits) and one edge each (darkvision, a narrow Defense bonus, a once-per-rest reroll or +2 after a failed check, or four activities). Each is fixed: what you see is what you get, with no choice made when you gain it. Humanoid foes keep their darkvision as plain-text senses, and drop these Runes like any other.

- **Uses sit on the power they limit**, as a tag on its activation (*Reaction (1/S.R.)*) or opening it (*2/S.R.*), never in the keywords, so a reader never wonders whether they cover the whole card. Whole-card pools (spell points, Resolve, Recipe flasks) stay where they are.
- **Every card is unique.** A name never carries its Rank: a stronger card gets a new name, not a numeral.
- Keep each card to **one clear idea** that fits on a card. The card says what it does: anything that overrides a rule (swap time, underwater use, size) is printed on it.
- **Size every number by Rank.** All bonuses stack, so a card's bonus or penalty equals its **Rank bonus**, and a card that attacks on its own uses a **choice of two abilities + its Rank bonus** (*Alchemist's Fire Recipe*, Rank II: DEX or INT + 3). Pick two abilities that serve different Callings; on a gated card they are its symbols. Spellbooks keep INT alone.
- **Taking a creature out of a fight costs at least 2 (Ced, 2026-11-17).** A spell or a WIL power that does it outright (**Subdue** with Unconscious, Stunned, Incapacitated or Charmed) is 2nd level at least, or costs at least 2 Resolve. A partial hold can cost 1: Restrained, Prone, Frightened, or Blinded for one turn (*Grease*, *Color Spray*, *Compel*). Breadth is what makes a deck strong, and a Rank I book of 4 points shouldn't end two scenes a day. So *Sleep* and *Charm Person* moved to 2nd level, and the *Enchanter's Book* gained *Heroism* (1st: can't be Frightened, 2 temporary HP at the start of each turn) to keep two 1st-level spells. No WIL power is affected yet (*Dread* Frightens, *Hex* Poisons).
- **Summons use a foe card (Ced, 2026-11-20).** *Raise Dead* (Necromancer's Book, 1st level) raises the corpse of a Small or Medium creature as a **Skeleton**: the foe card as printed, no new card to print. It acts on its side's turn right after the caster, who chooses its moves and actions; it dies at 0 HP; a caster keeps one (casting again makes the previous one collapse) and a corpse rises once; Duration 1 hour. Rejected: commanding it with the caster's action (too weak), its own initiative (bookkeeping), stats scaled by INT (math), stacking skeletons (a horde for spell points). It doubles the necromancer's actions for 1 spell point: watch it in playtests, and against the *Tactician*'s *Command* (same ground: an ally acts, not the same effect). The rest of the book is plain necromancy: *Chill Touch* (no healing), *Toll the Dead* (1d12 against the hurt), *False Life*, *Ray of Sickness*, *Vampiric Touch* (heals half, 2nd), *Blindness* (2nd: Blinded is a partial hold, so no Subdue). Same ground as the *Supplicant's Pact* (Necrotic, *Eclipse* blinds), different effects.
- **Trait budget (GM 4.3).** For Traits and Domains, **Rank = budget** (I 1 … V 5). A bonus (+1 modifier) costs 1, a perk 1, a strong perk 2, an activity ¼ (at most 4 per card, from at least two abilities). Never a lone bonus. Rank I has no bonus or perk (activities or one narrow edge). Families: Cross, Dual, Gifted (from Rank II). Concept cards in GM 4.4, random generator in GM 4.5. Techniques, weapons, Jewelry and other types are sized by the Rank table, not by the budget. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **Callings, not classes: encourage multiclassing.** Most cards light two affinities, so most loot fits most Callings. About **half** of all cards don't roll (flat bonuses, reactions, rerolls, movement, senses, uses); the other half (weapons, Spellbooks, Divine, Glamour and Nature, attack cards) roll the abilities of their symbols.
  - **Bridge cards** link two styles: a Technique that buffs allies when you hit (*Battle Hymn*), a weapon that strengthens magic (*Spellguard Staff*). Put some in every loot table.
- Give DEX and WIL real value. DEX already has a weapon Defense, initiative and ranged attacks. WIL has its Defense against charm and fear but few combat uses, so it needs social cards with teeth (allies, discounts, rallying, demoralizing) to be worth investing in. Divine, Glamour and Nature (P 9.5) are WIL's magic: buffs, debuffs, reactions, healing and Forms. Only Forms deal damage. The base set starts players on Glamour only (*Wanderer's Repertoire*, *Dancer's Bells*): the cleric and druid roles (Litany, Instinct, *Holy Amulet*) are loot, found in play. Social rewards are cards that take real Slots: favors (Consumable), contacts (Jewelry with uses), titles and renown (Domain).
- Give every card a type keyword that names its Slot (P 11.1). **Jewelry** holds worn magic (rings, amulets, cloaks, gloves, boots), usually active powers with limited uses; other passive bonuses belong in Runes.
- **Never print the same thing twice.** Keywords stay whole in `cards/base_set.py` (the sim and the rules read them), but `tools/build_cards.py` (`printed`) leaves out what the card's text already says: *Range* and *Thrown*, whose range is in the attack or casting line, and a Versatile weapon's die, which is in its damage.
- **The look by family.** Every card type has a symbol, printed at the bottom left and faint behind the text. Under the name, the family in grey: Calling, Equipment, Item, Rune, or Spell / Power on a deck card. The band marks the family: black for a Calling, a double rule under it for Equipment, hatched for an Item, the type symbol before the name for a Rune; a deck card prints its cover's symbol at the foot of its index (hatched for a Spellbook). Keywords sit at the foot on two lines: type and rule keywords, then descriptive ones in grey; each line shrinks to fit. A Rune prints "Rune of" (FR *Rune du / de la / de l' / des*) in small above its name, and its type alone under it (*Domain*); a deck cover, already named for its owner (*Wildheart's Instinct*), keeps *Rune · Nature*. Data: `RUNE_OF_THE` in `cards/base_set.py`, `RUNE_DE` in `cards/fr.py`. The Rank, on every card, is a branch rune carved in a stone at the top right (one branch per Rank, three on the right, then on the left): a counting rune keeps the runic look and still reads at a glance. The footers of a Calling and a Rune leave out the type, which the subtitle says. Type symbols are ink drawings in `rules/symbols/ink/` (made by `tools/gen_symbols.py` and `tools/ink_symbols.py`), with sketches in `rules/symbols/sketch/` until each is drawn.
- Armor adds mostly to STR Defense; Leather's +1 DEX Defense lets nimble characters trade 5 ft of speed for it or go unarmored to move faster. Mail and Hide weigh the same (+4 HP): Mail takes +3 STR Defense, Hide +2 and +5 ft, so the STR · WIL Bearer pays a point of Defense for speed instead of getting a weaker Mail.
- Card design should keep the **bookkeeping cost of a swap** low (how much math a swap triggers).
- **Never write on cards.** Two components are written on: the **tally card** (P 12.3), in pencil and disposable, and the **Bearer card** (P 2.5), in ink and kept for life. Everything else that changes is shown with tokens (uses) or with cards (gold cards in a Purse). Spell points and Resolve are counted on the tally card.
- **Name the card where the concept lives.** Rules speak of gear, Runes, Purses and bags of coins. Each concept that a card represents (Calling, gear, Rune, Runestone, Purse and gold, Spellbook, Litany, Repertoire and Instinct decks, Pack and Stash piles) gets one short "At the table…" sentence where the rules define it, pointing to P 12. Elsewhere, "card" appears only in the table-setup chapter (P 12). The rules stay playable with a plain sheet. Slots are objects as **Slot frames** (P 12.2): a frame holds a card and is never one, so "card" still means a Calling, gear, a Rune, a spell or gold.
- **Card layout (P 12.1):** name, symbols and Rank on top, bonus strip on the right, effect in the center, keywords at the bottom. The **affinity corner** is a 2 × 2 grid of the four ability symbols beside the Rank (STR DEX / INT WIL), the unused ones greyed out, so "can I equip this?" reads at a glance in a loot pile. The Calling card's strip holds only its own values. The **tally card** is the character sheet: card-sized, its strip lines up with the bonus strips and holds the ten totals; beside it, current HP (scratch box), temporary HP and death saves. Nothing derivable (attack bonuses, passive Awareness) goes on it. A card may have both a strip and an effect: players spread cards out to play and stack them (tally card first, then the Calling, each card on top one strip to the left, no vertical offset, so the rows line up) only to add up, so the strips read as a sum ending on the tally card. A swap is one subtraction and one addition.
- **Lore line.** Each card, if space permits, ends with a few sentences of lore at the bottom of its effect area, smaller and in italic. Lore never states or changes a rule, so dropping it loses nothing. It follows the lore constraints (1b): a Rune is told as a power and what it does to whoever carries it, never as a former owner, a Calling as who someone is, gear as an object someone made and carried. Keep it setting-light (no place names, no fixed answer to GM 6.1), one or two short sentences, about 25 words. The build shrinks the art first, then drops the lore when the card still overflows. Source: `LORE` in `cards/base_set.py`. Foe cards carry no lore: their space goes to the build.
- **Bearer card (P 2.5).** A separate double-width card, not the tally card's back: the tally card is worn out and replaced (and a Form takes a fresh one), so identity written there would be copied over again and again. Front: who (35 mm portrait box, name, age, folk, look); back: what drives the Bearer (personality, ambition, fear, three "name — bond" lines, background, notes). Plain labels, no prompt questions, so the card stays sober and leaves room to write. Ambition and fear are purely descriptive: no mechanic hangs on them, the GM uses them to build scenes (pillar: simple). "Folk" is the word for race or species on the card and in P 2.1: softer, and never a game term. One per player in the base set: it lasts, so no spares.
- **Tally card back: wishlist (TODO, with the card backs).** The back of the tally card holds 3 write-in lines for cards the player wants (GM 4.2). It tells the GM which drops will land without asking, and gives players a goal between stories. Not in PLAYERS.md or the card templates yet: add it when card backs are designed.
- **Slot frames (P 12.2).** Every Slot is a physical holder, so the build is a physical act and survives between sessions: **framed = equipped**, loose = Pack, Stash or ground. Frames make the Slot count visible (no "how many Rune Slots do I have?"), and growth is a moment at the table: the GM hands over a new Rune frame at each milestone and a unique frame (Third Hand, Second Jewel, Great Backpack) from a Boss. Extra Gear Slot frames never go into the Pack. Runestones use the same frame, so capture, etch and swap are the same gesture. Specs: **67 × 98 mm** for a 63 × 88 mm card, home-printed on unsleeved cardstock, 8 per landscape Letter page; top band of 8 mm (Slot symbol, name, swap time), 2.5 mm left border, 1.5 mm right border so the card's strip sits on the frame's edge and framed cards stack to add up (P 12.3); **two photo-corner slits**, top right (cut into the band) and bottom left, opposite corners so the card can't slide out in storage; the center, hidden under the card, prints what an empty Slot does (the Armor frame prints +10 Speed in its strip row). Decks frame only their cover; a Consumable stack shares one frame. Source: `cards/base_set.py`, built by `tools/build_cards.py`.
- **Special Slots (future, not in the rules).** A special Slot is a frame of the same type, found as loot with a Rank, that **replaces** one of your plain frames. Its bonus belongs to the **Slot**, not the card, so it boosts whatever card sits in it and stays with your body when you change gear (*a Hand frame that gives +2 Attack to its weapon*). It prints its bonus in the top band and in the center. Open: whether special frames can be traded or lost, how strong they get by Rank, and whether the bonus may go in a strip (the frame's right border would have to widen into a second strip).
- **One word, one meaning.** The vocabulary lives in `GLOSSARY.md` (repo root): each term has one meaning, and its _Avoid_ words are never used for it. The handbooks' Glossary chapters are generated from it (`tools/sync_glossary.py`, run by the PDF build); edit the glossary there, never in the handbooks.
- **Keywords carry the rules.** When several cards share a mechanic, make it a keyword instead of repeating text, and add it to P Chapter 11. Never reuse a reserved word with another meaning. Keyword order: type, properties, uses.
- **Conditional bonuses** (*+2 STR Defense against poison*) go in the effect text, never in the strip: the strip holds only what always applies.
- **Spellbook decks (P 12.4):** the cover card sits in front of the fan; spell cards fan behind it. Each spell card has a **left-edge index** that stays visible in a hand fan or a sideways spread: spell point cost at the top corner, casting-time and target-Defense icons, the name running down the edge, and the book's name. Order spells by cost. Keep a deck small enough to fan in one hand (cover + 6 to 10 spells). Litanies, Repertoires and Instincts follow the same layout (Resolve cost at the corner); an Instinct holds 2–4 Forms by Rank.
- **Foe cards (GM 2.1).** A monster is built from player cards: its card prints a plain-text body (modifiers, HP, Speed, size) and names its gear and Runes in *italics*, by name and **card ID**, and the build adds up totals and attacks (`cards/foes.py`, built by `tools/build_cards.py` into the base set with blank foe cards). Write only what no card could be as **plain text** (anatomy, a curse, giant strength): it never drops, and it may change how the monster's italic cards perform (extra dice, extra HP) while the card that drops stays the normal one. A foe card **includes its body**: another body is another foe card. Bosses carry the **Boss** tag, group leaders the **Leader** keyword (Morale, GM 2.4). Foe cards are **double width** (126 × 88 mm, 3 per Letter page): the left half is a drawing (`card_art.FOE_ART`), the right half a normal card with its strip on the right edge, so a tucked card's strip still lines up. The drop line names each card in full with its ID. A foe may use a few powers of a deck it carries (`powers`); the whole deck drops. Card text on a foe card prints the foe's numbers (*WIL + 2* → *+5*). Keep plain text rare: every power a player watches being used against them should be a card they could win.
- **Classic monsters (GM 2.2).** Built like the rest: italic cards that drop, plain text for anatomy. *Undead* (plain text): resists Poison and Necrotic, vulnerable to Radiant, never checks Morale. *Defiance* (Trait, Rank II) is the card behind "won't stay down" (Orc, Zombie). The Troll's *Regeneration* and claws are plain text; at 80 HP and two 2d6 + 4 claws it sits beside the Ogre as a brute. *Provisional: tune the Troll and the Zombie in the sim.*
- **Card IDs (P 12.1):** type letter, Rank, number in order of appearance in `cards/base_set.py` (*W-II-02*). Letters: L Calling (no Rank: *L-01*) · A Armor · W Weapon · S Shield · M Instrument · J Jewelry · P Consumable · R Recipe · C Container · B Spellbook · T Trait · Q Technique · K Domain · D Divine · G Glamour · N Nature. Add new cards **at the end** of their type and Rank so existing IDs never change.

- **Starter expansion (decided 2026-11-12).** Ced asked to double the starter list with the beginner tools of 5e's classes and backgrounds: 81 starter cards instead of 43. Fighting styles, class signatures and backgrounds became Rank I Runes; four Spellbooks (Evoker, Abjurer, Illusionist, Enchanter, max 2nd level, 6–7 spells each) join the *Conjurer's Book*; the Litany and the Instinct moved from loot to the starter list, with the *Shortbow*, *Greataxe*, *Greatclub*, *Wand* and *Holy Amulet* (they keep their affinities). Changes made to respect the perk rule (section 4): *Sneak Attack* dropped (it was *Sure Thing*), *Favored Enemy* dropped (damage on a hit and a mark: *Spell Strike*, *Quarry*), *Dueling*, *Thrown* and *Rage* lost their +2 damage (the same effect as *Spell Strike* at INT +2) and give Defense, range or resistance instead; *Lay on Hands* dropped (healing stays WIL magic's, section 3); *Two-Weapon Fighting* and *Flurry of Blows* left for the DEX maneuvers. The background Domain for the acolyte was dropped (it was *Healing* word for word). *Command* the spell is *Compel*, so it doesn't share the Tactician's perk name. More Spellbooks than WIL decks is on purpose: variety is the INT caster's identity, the WIL caster's is the short rest (section 3).
- **The Pact is WIL magic that deals damage (decided 2026-11-12).** A fourth WIL deck type, same rules as the others: Dirges and Cues. Ced chose that it hurts: *Eldritch Blast* (Dirge, 0 Resolve, 1d10 Necrotic against WIL Defense) is the only damage in WIL magic outside Forms, and *Hellish Rebuke* (Cue) adds 1d10 Fire to its −2. No power adds damage to a hit, so it duplicates no perk. Watch it against the *Conjurer's Book*'s *Fire Bolt*: same die, but a short-rest pool and no Hand.

## 5. Design Notes
- **Runestones are the Rune economy.** A loose Rune fades within a minute, so every Rune kept costs a blank Runestone, a Pack or Stash card, and (when looted) 1 action within a minute of the kill. This turns loot choice into a real decision at the body, not in the Stash weeks later, and keeps the Stash from filling with ordinary drops. Numbers: 3 carried Runestones per character (bulky, raised by a Container that costs a Pack piece of its own), 2 per character in the Stash, 250 gp each (a Rank I card costs 100 gp: a blank Runestone is worth more than an ordinary Rune, so players release junk instead of hoarding it). Pack 8 = 3 Runestones + the old 5 gear cards; Stash 5 per character (upgrades 8/12/20, the old 10/15/25/40 ratios) so it scales with party size. Release is free and swaps need no extra Runestone, so a long-rest rebuild never stalls on Runestone count. Capture skips the Pack cost so it stays a 1-action choice in combat (finish an enemy or save its Rune).
- **Gold is gear.** A Purse holds gold up to a Rank cap (100 / 500 / 2,000 / 10,000 / no limit) and costs 1/10 of its capacity (10 / 50 / 200 / 1,000 / 5,000 gp); its gold takes no room, loose bags of coins take 1 piece each. Paying needs the Purse nearby, so the party chooses who carries the money and where it sits (Pack, Stash), with exposure to theft and disaster left to the GM. The starting *Coin Pouch* (Rank I) takes 1 of the 8 Pack pieces. Gold cards (1, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1,000 gp) keep the amount on the table without writing.
- **One engine, one Slot.** Every build's engine costs one Slot: a weapon a Hand, a Spellbook an Item (plus an empty Hand to cast), a Divine, Glamour or Nature Rune a Rune Slot. The WIL engine costs a growing Slot (1 of 3 at the start, 1 of 8 at the peak) but frees a Hand and an Item. Rune Slots are the one shared growth track, so parity comes from loot: every style gets an equal share of Runes at each Rank (GM 4.1). *Playtest: if WIL builds lag at 3 Rune Slots, revisit (a dedicated magic Slot or a 4th starting Rune Slot were the alternatives).*
- **INT magic rests long, WIL magic rests short (decided 2026-11-12).** This is how 5e balances its casters, and we keep it: the wizard gets the widest list and pays with a book and long-rest slots; the warlock gets few slots that return on a short rest; the cleric and druid get the healing. Here, Spellbooks carry the **variety** (more books, damage, areas, utility), cost an Item Slot and an empty Hand, can be lost or stolen, and their points return on a **long rest**. WIL decks are fewer and narrower, but Resolve returns on a **short rest**, needs no Hand, and holds every Mend. So a set may print more Spellbooks than WIL decks without breaking parity: compare points per day, not card counts. No extra price on books (an armor penalty was considered and dropped): the empty Hand and the long rest are the price. A pact, the 5e warlock, is WIL magic for the same reason: it is short-rest magic.
- **WIL magic is a Rune, INT magic is gear.** A Spellbook is a book you hold; prayer, art and the instinct of beasts are powers you carry. So WIL magic swaps only at a long rest and lives in Runestones; Instruments and other **Focus** gear are the fast-swap, stealable part.
- **Swap times.** Fast Slots (Hand, Jewelry, Item) keep fights tactical; slow Slots (Armor, Rune) fix the build between rests. The Pack cost (action + all movement) makes mid-fight swaps a real trade-off; the expended lock stops cycling spent jewels between fights (spent spell points stay on the tally card whatever book you hold).
- **Subdue replaces Legendary Resistance and saves (P 8).** The target loses its next turn; then the source repeats the attack (full bonus, so stacking helps it last too) with decay: one disadvantage die per repeat that hit and per time the source took damage, kept by a die beside the target. Design target: a Subdue lasts 2–3 turns, rarely 4 (under 20%). In the sim (`sim/dev/subdue.js`, a Rank I Stunning spell on the toughest foe, 7 QA fights, 40 seeds, 3d20 cap): regular foes lost 1 turn 53%, 2 turns 38%, 3 turns 9%, 4+ 0% (avg 1.6), and two-thirds of them died while held (so real holds run longer than that); a Boss lost avg 2 turn-or-half-turns (about one round), 4+ 10%. The win rate didn't move (55–59%). The old rule (bare-modifier maintain rolls, dice per hit on the source and the target, weakening) lasted 1 turn 57% of the time and needed three counters. Spending both actions to hold on (skip a decay step) barely mattered in the sim (+0.2 turns): kept as a choice for a caster with nothing better to do. A non-Subdue impairing effect makes one attack roll and lasts what it prints (2 turns for Web's Restrained and Dread's Frightened, 1 minute for Bane): no maintain rolls at all.
- **High spells cost more instead of once a day (P 9.2).** 5th level costs 6 points and 6th costs 8: the pool is the brake, with no per-book flag to track. A Rank III book (+9, max 5th) with INT +4 gives 13 points: one 5th-level spell and 7 left; a Rank V book (+18) with INT +5 gives 23: two 6th-level spells, plus 7.
- **Spell power per point stays flat (P 9.2; decided 2026-11-15).** The brake is the action, not the pool: a caster casts about one spell a turn, 3–4 per fight, so 12 points of 1-point spells is steady value over three fights, and 12 points in one spell is one turn. A big spell is worth its cost because its power lands in a single action; if it were also worth more per point it would be strictly better. Sizing rules, in "1st-level units" (one Rank I single-target spell, about 10 damage: *Magic Missile*):
  - **Single target: power ≈ cost × one 1st-level unit, never more.** Cost 2 ≈ 20, cost 4 ≈ 40, cost 8 ≈ 80 (*Disintegrate*, 75).
  - **Areas and control are what high levels buy.** An area may hit each creature for less (about 3.5 units per target at cost 6, like *Cone of Cold*), and a high spell may do what no small one can (fly, banish, a wall).
  - **The floor is the cantrip.** A 1-point spell is worth about two cantrip hits (cantrip 1d10 ≈ 5.5); a cantrip at Rank I never beats 1d10 on one target.
  - **Upcasting pays less per point** than the spell's base level: it is the fallback when no better spell fits (*Magic Missile*: +1 dart, about 3.5, per level).
  Comparison with fifth edition: there, single-target damage per point of the spell-point variant (costs 2/3/5/6/7/9 for 1st–6th) stays near 5 at every level, and high levels gain through areas and unique effects. Our curve 1/2/3/4/6/8 is steeper (5e scaled to 1st = 1 reads 1 / 1.5 / 2.5 / 3 / 3.5 / 4.5), so high spells cost more of the pool here; that is on purpose, since the once-a-day cap of 6th level and above is gone. *Check the existing spells against these rules; the full-day nova stays open (section 5, end).*
- **One pool each, on the tally card (P 9.2, 9.5, 12.3).** Spell points = the **best** equipped book + INT (minimum 1); Resolve = the **best** WIL deck (Divine, Glamour, Nature or Pact) + WIL (minimum 1). "Best" is the one that prints the most. Extra points from Callings, Traits and gear add to the total, with no pool to choose. The tally card's box reads *total – marks*: write the total, draw one mark per point spent, erase at the rest. This replaces tokens on deck covers and per-deck pools: one number to track, WIL and INT counted once. A book or deck can come off while the points spent still fit in the new total, so the expended lock covers uses only; points belong to no book, so swapping books moves only the total.
- **A second book or deck adds breadth, never fuel (Ced, 2026-11-17).** Until then every equipped book and every WIL deck added its points: a second book gave +2 points and six spells for an Item Slot whose rivals are a potion or a pouch, and three WIL decks gave 9 + WIL Resolve per short rest. Now a second book is its spells ready without a swap (from the Pack in combat: both actions, plus 1 action to equip it, P 4.5), and a second deck is its powers for a Rune Slot, a fair trade against a Technique. A WIL deck in a Rune Slot outclasses a Trait on purpose: it is the engine (see *One engine, one Slot*), and it takes no Hand and no Item. Considered and dropped: one book or deck at a time (kills variety), a pool by Rank on the tally card (reprints every cover), a capped sum (one more calculation). In the sim the sample party doesn't move (one book, one deck each); only two-book and two-deck builds lose points. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **No costly components.** Powerful spells are gated by the Spellbook's Rank and by loot instead of gold.
- **No concentration**, for snappiness, and no Lasting effects: only Subdue repeats its roll. Without a one-spell limit, buffs stack; keep their durations short (1 minute = 10 rounds) when converting, and watch stacked blessings in playtests.
- **Trials, not fights, are the unit of play.** The play loop (P 1), the loot budget (GM 4.1) and the milestone pace all count trials, and a fight is one kind. A talked-out or snuck-past trial pays the same as a won fight, so no player is pushed into combat to get loot, and quiet builds (WIL, INT, DEX) earn as much as attack builds. The XP budget (GM 5) sizes fights only, since non-combat trials are sized by DC and reward.
- **Story → Quest → Trial.** Each scale has its own payoff: cards per trial, an optional quest reward per quest, a milestone per story. Milestones are party-wide only (no personal goals), so the loop always pulls the party forward together and no character's Slot count pulls ahead. The loop is named "play loop", not "core loop", to keep the old Core term out, and its unit is a "trial" because Challenge Rating already uses "challenge". Exploration lives at the quest scale, not in the loop: it has no mechanism of its own, and every loop step is a moment of play (a roll, a loot choice, a build change).
- **Everything stacks, and dice limit themselves.** Each extra d20 is worth less than the one before. Flat-bonus equivalent of each die at a 50% base: +5.0, +2.5, +1.3 (25% base: +3.8, +2.8, +2.1). Totals: 2d20 ≈ +5, 3d20 ≈ +7.5, 4d20 ≈ +9, so dice never run away the way flat bonuses can. Kept-die spread shrinks (SD 5.8 → 4.7 → 3.9 → 3.3), so under advantage each flat +1 is worth up to 7–8% instead of 5%. The problem is never the stacking but cheap, always-on sources: control where dice come from, not how far they stack. Raising every bonus or Defense to compensate was rejected: unadvantaged attacks fall to 20–30%, every turn becomes a hunt for dice, maintain rolls (bare modifier) fall behind, and the numbers grow.
- **Crits scale with dice.** Chance of a natural 20 on the kept die: 5% · 9.8% · 14.3% · 18.5% (1–4d20). Price "on a critical hit" effects for stacked attacks.
- **Outnumbered compares sides, around the target only.** One die to the side with more creatures around the target: the attacker's side engaged with the target (attacker included) against the target and its allies within 5 ft of it. Counting the target's allies "engaged with your side" anywhere on the map was a chained lookup; now the GM looks at one spot, and a second orc helps only by standing beside the first, so holding a line means standing shoulder to shoulder. Not one die per creature: counting the difference on every attack was the slowest step at the table, and the yes/no comparison is a glance. Everyone attacking the same target gets the same dice, so a back-line archer or caster gains nothing over the melee (counting only the attacker's allies would give a non-engaged attacker one extra die). A lone attacker at range against an unengaged target: 0 against 0, no dice. Rewards holding a line and fighting in pairs; a bigger crowd adds nothing more.
- **Monsters share the players' cards.** The party sees a power used against it, then can win the very card: enemies with strong flavor become a shopping list, and the GM never converts loot. Building a monster is choosing cards, so a custom foe takes minutes (tuck a loot card behind a foe card, or fill a blank one). The foe card never leaves the GM; drops are copies pulled from the loot pool by ID, so a monster can be replayed forever. Foe cards stay hidden: what a monster carried is learned when it falls. Uses reset each fight so nothing is tracked between trials. "Monstrous" was removed: plain text on the foe card does its job without a keyword, and only italic cards drop, so six legionaries don't drop six pila (the trial's loot budget picks).
- **Open: foe difficulty.** How many cards and which Ranks make a fair foe, and whether CR and XP should be computed from them (like the Trait budget), is undecided. For now foe cards print no CR or XP (keywords instead: Foe, Boss, size, senses); the GM judges CR (GM 5). No roles (minion, elite): rejected for now.
- **Wounds replace Exhaustion (P 7.6).** A Wound at every fall to 0 HP makes HP matter beyond the fight: the death-save loop stays, but going down now costs something that lasts. Only downtime (3 days in a safe place) heals them, so the party's distance from safety becomes a resource the GM controls, and quests gain a natural return trip. No maximum: the penalty grows until a Bearer is no longer worth fielding, which pushes retreat and care, not death. The deck (20 cards, shared, no duplicates while drawn): 12 light penalties (one number or one roll type), 5 Crippling (a Hand, Jewelry or Item Slot; never Rune or Armor, which would force losing a Rune without a Runestone or putting Armor in the Pack), 3 grave (two penalties, or −10 HP). Penalties print in the strip when they hit a total, so they stack like gear. Hazards (cold, hunger, thirst) give Wounds; drowning takes you to 0 HP. Healing magic never touches Wounds: only downtime and rare remedies (*Restorative Salve*, Rank II Consumable; later a Rank IV Mend). Monsters never take Wounds.
- **Runes go Dormant; Tend or heal (P 7.5; decided 2026-11-15).** Like the weapons that break in *Breath of the Wild*, a Rune is something you use up and replace, not a fixed part of you. Three aims: the fiction (a Rune never anchors in a Bearer, so it is borrowed, not owned), Runes as a resource (a captured Rune is either etched or burned to wake another), and a real choice at each rest. Rule: at any rest you can Tend (no HP back, everything else returns); if a rest heals you, roll 1d8 (short) or 1d4 (long), and on a 1 the player picks one etched Rune to go Dormant (face down, nothing works, keeps its Slot, can be etched out into a Runestone and stays Dormant there). Wake at a long rest by releasing a Runestone holding a Rune of equal or higher Rank. Downtime and healing outside a rest (potions, Mends, First Aid) never roll, so healers and potions gain value and a safe place stays a reward. Odds: a Bearer who heals at both short rests and the long rest loses a Rune on about 43% of days (1 − (7/8)² × 3/4), about 0.5 Runes a day on average; healing only at the long rest, 25%. High Ranks are fragile on purpose: a Dormant Rank IV or V Rune needs a Runestone of the same Rank, so its bearer Tends more often, and the dilemma grows with power (pillar: the Rank IV–V chase). The player picks which Rune goes Dormant (agency, no bad-luck build wreck); the roll is a bare die, but cards may change it later (design space: a Trait that steadies Runes, a curse that loosens them). Economy unchanged (250 gp blank Runestone, 3 in the Pack); GM 4.1 keeps Rank I Runes dropping as fuel. *Playtest: watch whether parties simply skip healing at short rests, and whether Rune churn outpaces drops.*
- **Outnumbered vs area attacks.** Outnumbered (P 6.5) rewards ganging up on one enemy. Counter it by giving most monsters at least one area attack (a sweep, a burst, a breath) so clustering around a single target has a cost. To be made explicit later in a "Building monsters and balanced fights" section of the GM's Guide.
- **Information design: public rules, partly hidden state, announced intentions.** Every rule a foe uses is public: its keywords (Boss, Leader, Recharge 5–6) are in P 11.6 and Morale in the player glossary (P 14; the GM runs it, GM 2.4), so players can plan around them. The state is partly hidden: foe cards stay with the GM, and what a monster carries is learned when it falls, by spying (GM 2.3: one line, the strip, or the whole card on a big success, one try per spy and group, a failed hide costs an ambush or a cut-off spy) or by watching it act. Intentions are announced: a recharged power is described when it comes back (GM 2.1), so a big hit is a threat the party sees coming and can answer (spread out, Dodge, kill it first), never a surprise from nowhere.
- **Recharge rolls at the end of the monster's turn.** Same odds as at the start, but the roll now lands before the Bearers act, so the GM can telegraph the ready power (the ogre draws back its club) and the players get a side-turn to react. At the start of its turn it would fire the moment it came back. A Boss's half turn doesn't roll, so the half turn can't double the recharge rate.
- **Morale makes target priority matter.** In the bandit sims (`build/bandits/REPORT.md`, F5), killing the Bandit Captain first was no better than killing the nearest bandit: a leader's death removed only its own damage. With Morale (WIL check DC 10 when the Leader falls and at half the group down; now one roll for the group with its best WIL, so the GM rolls once and the fight usually ends or goes on whole), Captain-first went from mid-table to the top two (+25–35 win points), most sound plans gained 5–15 points, fights got ~1 round shorter, and bad plans (splitting up, chasing archers) stayed bad. A Boss never checks, so Boss fights keep their length. Side effect: fights get easier; size Leader groups about one step harder (to add to GM 5). Fleeing or surrendering is the GM's call, so a fled foe can return, bargain or be questioned.
- **Downed creatures don't engage (P 6.5).** An Incapacitated creature is engaged with no one either way, so foes next to a downed Bearer stop counting for Outnumbered and its allies aren't shot at 4d20 keep lowest. A clarification, worth +2–4 win points to everyone in the sims.
- **Initiative is simple, and a sneak always pays off (P 6.1).** A side that sneaks up unnoticed goes first with full turns, no roll: the party's effort at stealth (or the GM's foes sneaking past a careless party) can't be undone by a bad die. Surprise no longer rolls with disadvantage. Otherwise each side adds its **best DEX**: one number to look up, no averaging or rounding, and the scout's DEX matters. Going first isn't automatically good under the first-mover rule, so a high-DEX party gains little in an open fight; the payoff is in sneaking. All-Invisible sides keep their advantage die on the roll (P 8).
- **Three questions (P 6.6).** Situational dice were one per source, Outnumbered one per creature, which meant counting before every attack. Now You, Target and Crowd each give one die at most, so situations reach 3 dice at most (4d20). In the sim (`sim/dev/batch.js`, the 7 QA fights, 100 seeds, same policy) the change is close to neutral and slightly harder on the party: PC wins Road 83 → 80, Wolves 100 → 100, Ogre 95 → 97, Chieftain alone 83 → 79, Sample Boss 28 → 22, Goblins 17 → 17, Legionaries 26 → 26. The policy rarely stacked more than one die per question, so the change is mostly about table speed, not balance.

- **Advantage on damage (P 6.6).** Once you'd hit anyway, extra attack dice are wasted; moving them to damage gives them a use, adds a choice to advantaged attacks, and lets damage builds lean on advantage. Cancel first, then split; choose before rolling; disadvantage always stays on the attack (else every penalty softens); the moved die rerolls the whole damage, crit dice included; area damage rerolls once for every target; only dice move, never flat bonuses. Break-even (expected damage, crits ignored; attack dice also raise crits): a die on damage pays once the single-d20 hit chance reaches about 80–85%, i.e. hitting on a natural 4–5: 1d12+3 79%, 1d8+3 83%, 1d6+3 85%, 2d10+4 85%, 3d10+5 87% (the flat part grows with Rank, so the threshold creeps up). Hence the table rule "worth it when you'd hit on a 5 or better". In the sim (`sim/dev/batch.js`, 100 seeds, both sides split for the best expected damage) 14% of leftover dice moved and every fight stayed within ±1 win of keeping them on the attack: at Rank I, hit chances rarely reach 80%. A "move at 75%" policy moved 32% and cost the party (Chieftain alone 90 → 84 wins). Removing the 3d20 cap changed no fight result; Subdue holds stayed about 1.5 turns (regular) and 2 (Boss). Expect the option to matter at higher Ranks and against off-Defenses.
- **No stacking cap.** Limited Slots are the limit for bonuses: a card spent on one number isn't spent elsewhere. Dice need no cap: only the three questions give them, so an attack rolls 4d20 at most; only decay grows past that, and it ends the effect. Offense saturates (a natural 1 caps a single d20 at 95%, so stacking to-hit beyond ~90% is wasted). Advantage dice break that cap: a natural 1 counts only on the kept die, so 3d20 against an easy Defense hits about 99%, and advantage on damage (P 6.6) gives those dice a use. Lopsided defenders are answered by the GM choosing another angle: a STR/DEX stacker gets attacked on INT or WIL, or with area effects; any stacker has other Defenses. Fight-ending spells are limited by Subdue's decay.
- **Two actions and a reaction (P 6.2).** Movement is an action (Move, split around the other action), so Dash is gone, and there is no bonus action. Every choice is one currency: moving costs an attack, so positioning, range and hit-and-run carry real weight. Attack and Cast can each be taken twice (no limit on spells per turn). Old bonus actions became an **action** when they heal, shift or buff the group (potions, *Second Wind*, *Healing Word*, Anthems, Forms) and a **reaction** otherwise (flasks, *Steady Aim*, *Shield Bash*, *Misty Step*, Bellow): a quick trick on your turn costs your opportunity attack or your defensive reaction until your next turn. Two-weapon fighting is a reaction. *Twin Strike* adds one attack once per turn (3 at most). Disengage costs an action and is impossible when surrounded (3+ enemies engaged with you): retreating from even one foe costs an attack, so hit-and-run is a real trade, and surrounded matches the Crowd die (3 against 1). Standing up takes an action. The first-mover rule and the Boss half turn are one action.
- **What the switch did in the sim** (`sim/dev/batch.js`, the 7 QA fights, 40 seeds, same policy): fights got 25–40% shorter, and Bearer damage per round rose 30–65% (not ×2: whoever moves loses an attack). Standing still pays most: casters (two Magic Missiles a turn), archers and slingers (Goblins +65%: 2 vs 6 Goblins fell from 15 to 8 wins out of 40). Lone big foes fell behind: the Chieftain alone went from 20 to 29 party wins out of 40, the Ogre dies in about 3 rounds, because four Bearers attacking twice each multiply Outnumbered. Heavy weapons gain the most standing still (Greatsword 6.8 → 13.7 expected per turn at Defense 13); a Light pair falls from 93% to 77% of a Greatsword and now spends the reaction. Loading loses most of its cost (one shot per action).
- **What the pillar pass did in the sim** (3d20 cap, local Outnumbered, group Morale, Disengage, Subdue; `sim/dev/batch.js`, 40 seeds, same seeds before and after): PC wins Road 31 → 33, Wolves 40 → 40, Ogre 40 → 40, Chieftain alone 31 → 35, Sample Boss 11 → 8, Goblins 8 → 4, Legionaries 7 → 17. The big mover is the legion: its three Legionaries stand 10 ft apart, so under the local count they no longer back each other up, and one failed Morale roll now breaks the whole group. Foes that should fight as a line must stand shoulder to shoulder (add to GM 5). Two Bearers against six Goblins got harder: the goblins crowd one target while the pair can't help each other unless they stand side by side.
- **What Callings did in the sim** (`sim/dev/batch.js --seeds 40 --by`, same seeds, before = Cores at HEAD): the sample party became Templar (Bram), Infiltrator (Wren), Sage (Isolde), Performer (Aldo) with the same cards; foes kept their exact totals. PC wins: Road 33 → 37, Wolves 40 → 40, Ogre 40 → 40, Chieftain alone 34 → 36, Sample Boss 9 → 12, Goblins (2 vs 6) 4 → 1, Legionaries 20 → 27. PC deaths over all fights 53 → 17: the HP on Armor and Traits (Bram 14 → 22) keeps Bearers up. Damage moved to the Sage (staff and book on INT, +1 spell point: Ogre 14.6 → 17.9 DPR); the Templar and Infiltrator lost a point of their main ability (+2 instead of the Brute's and Nimble's +3), which sinks the two-Bearer Goblin fight (Wren 5.7 → 3.1 DPR). *Open:* starting HP is now a bit high for the old encounter budget, and +2 / +2 weakens a one-ability specialist; options are a Calling at +3 / +1 (the player picks) or +1 to-hit on Rank I weapons. Oath and Crowd Work are used whenever the policy can.
- **Open after the switch:** raise Boss and brute HP (about +30–50%), watch the casters' two-spell nova over a whole adventuring day (single fights don't show it), give Light weapons an edge of their own (done: Flurry), and give maps more cover against stationary shooters. Retest with `node sim/dev/batch.js --by`.

## 7. Order of Definitions (Player Rules)

Rule: a term is never used before it is defined. Its first appearance is bold and carries its definition, at least in one phrase; the full rule may come later, with a section number. Only three reference tables may be pointed at ahead of time, always by number: conditions (P 8), keywords and types (P 11), damage types (P 7.2). Chapters follow the tree below: each line needs only the lines above it.

├─ Rune, Runestone, etch · cards: Calling, gear, Runes · Slot (named)   P 2.1
├─ ability > modifier > Defense · HP · Speed        P 2.2-2.3
│  └─ totals > tally card                           P 2.3
├─ Calling > affinities, effects, powers            P 2.4
├─ dice > check, DC > attack roll                   P 3.1
│  └─ advantage, disadvantage > situation, condition   P 3.1
├─ turn > action, reaction, round, free interaction · short rest, long rest, downtime   P 3.2
├─ passive Awareness · Bright, Dark > hiding        P 3.3-3.5
├─ card > Rank, affinities, bonuses, effect, power, use, keyword, type   P 4.1
│  └─ Slot kinds                                    P 4.2
│     └─ Pack, piece, Container, Party Stash, gold, Purse   P 4.3
