# BEARERS — Design Rules
*Internal. Not built to PDF. Constraints for writing cards, Callings, monsters and spells, the reasons behind the rules, and fifth-edition conversion. "(P 6.5)" points to the Player Rules, "(GM 3)" to the Game Master's Guide.*

---

## 1. Pillars
Three rules pillars, **in priority order**. When two conflict, the higher one wins.

1. **Snappy and simple.** Simpler than D&D: few rules, few numbers to track, fast turns. A rule earns its place only if it changes decisions at the table; anything done every turn or every attack (a count, a lookup, a token) must stay a glance.
2. **Strategic.** Bad decisions get punished. Positioning, range, target priority, hit-and-run and surrounding carry real weight. A tactical rule is added only if it stays simple: one count, one number, no exceptions to memorize.
3. **Everything stacks.** Every bonus, penalty and die adds up, with as few exceptions as possible. An exception is allowed only when stacking would slow play (pillar 1) or flatten choices (pillar 2), and each one is listed below with its reason.

Also: interactive · lots of character customization · balanced. Power comes from a fixed Calling + Aspects + gear; characters have no Rank or level, and all growth is cards (and Slots).

**Stacking exceptions** (the full list; add any new one here):
- A condition doesn't stack with itself, and a power doesn't stack with itself (P 3.6, 8): repeat casts would otherwise need per-instance tracking (1).
- No two copies of the same card (P 3.6): one card, one effect, keeps Slots a choice (2).
- Situations give one die per question (You, Target, Crowd), however many reasons each has (P 5.2): three glances instead of a count (1). Combining kinds of tactics still pays, piling one kind on doesn't (2).
- Temporary HP keep the higher amount (P 7.3); two resistances to one type count as one (P 7.2): balance (Slots spent on survival would make a Bearer untouchable).
- A roll is rerolled only once (P 11.4): stops chains of Cues (1).
- Area rolls ignore each target's situations and Outnumbered (P 6.6): one roll for everyone (1), and clustering has a cost (2).
- Subdue repeats ask no questions (P 8): only decay dice, one count beside the target (1).
- Outnumbered gives one die however big the difference (P 6.5): the Crowd question (1).
- A Form ignores gear (P 10.7): it's a second body, not a stack on the first.

## 1b. Lore Constraints
- Player characters are **Bearers** (P 1): they absorb **Aspects**; everything else is **gear**, a real object. Each is born to a **Calling** (the pair of abilities they're gifted in), which is never absorbed and never lost; only a Rank V Aspect rewrites it. A Bearer's **look and personality never change**, except that a Form (P 10.7) changes the shape for a while. A shifted Bearer always reverts and keeps their mind.
- Write Aspects as something a being *is* or *knows*, a Calling as who someone is, and the other types as objects someone could pick up.

## 2. Power Ceiling
Rank IV cards, 8 Aspects and all three extra Gear Slots are the **peak**, roughly a level-12 hero in fifth edition. Rank IV is slightly game-breaking; Rank V is meta and game-breaking, a Boss treasure players hunt for.

Power scaling comes mainly from Rank (better cards). The Calling never grows: Armor and Traits carry the HP growth the Core used to (section 4, Callings). Slot count grows slowly: 3 → 8 Aspects, plus at most three extra Gear Slots. Five milestones (one per story; a story is 3–5 quests of 3–5 trials, about 15 trials) ≈ fifth edition to level 12.

## 3. Rank Scaling
| Rank | Bonus | Power reference | Spellbook (typical; + INT on the tally card) | Armor HP (light / medium / heavy) | Trait HP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | +2 | A small edge: a narrow check bonus, a basic weapon or armor, a minor trait | +2 points, max 2nd | +2 / +4 / +6 | +2 |
| **II** | +3 | A defining feature: an extra attack, heavy armor, a reliable power | +5 points, max 3rd | +4 / +8 / +12 | +4 |
| **III** | +4 | A strong feature: a magic weapon or armor, a signature power | +9 points, max 5th | +7 / +14 / +21 | +7 |
| **IV** | +5 | Slightly game-breaking: a legendary item, a power that bends a core rule | +14 points, max 6th | +10 / +20 / +30 | +10 |
| **V** | +6 | Game-breaking: changes the balance of play (meta Aspects, rule-breaking gear, size beyond Large, a new Calling) | +18 points, max 6th, plus one rule-breaking power | +14 / +28 / +42 | +14 |

*HP columns provisional until the sim pass.* A **body** of a given Rank (a Form, a foe built from scratch) keeps the old Core sizing: modifiers total +4 / +6 / +8 / +10 (max +3 / +4 / +5 / +5) and 12 / 30 / 58 / 90 HP at Rank I–IV.

- **Divine, Glamour and Nature (P 10.6)** are WIL's magic, and each is an Aspect type. Resolve = printed + WIL (minimum 1); printed by Rank: I 3 · II 4 · III 5 · IV 6 · V 7, back on a short rest. Anthems, Cues and Forms don't roll, so anyone can splash them; WIL buys **more uses**, not more power per use. Powers cost 0–3. Anthem and Dirge bonuses and penalties equal the Rank bonus; Rank III+ powers may grant an advantage or disadvantage die. Each type has one identity: **Glamour** gives flat bonuses and penalties (the Rank bonus); **Divine** alters fate with rerolls and fate dice (d4 at I–II, d6 at III, d8 at IV–V) and heals; **Nature** shifts and heals. Divine and Nature heal most; a Repertoire carries one Mend at most (*Rousing Verse*), so a Glamour starter can patch an ally up without becoming the healer. Watch healing: a Templar or Performer (WIL +2) with a Rank I Litany has 5 Resolve, three Mends per short rest. **Mends** cost at least 2, since Resolve comes back on a short rest and a short rest already heals half your missing HP; heal by Rank like a 5e spell of the deck's max level. Anthems stack like every bonus: keep them narrow (one roll type) and watch two-performer parties in playtests. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **Forms (P 10.7)** are bodies, printed like a Calling's strip. Cost 1 (utility: scouting, flying, swimming), 2 (fighting) or 3 (brutes). STR and DEX are sized like a body of the Remembrance's Rank (section 3: Rank I total about +4, max +3). HP pool by cost: ⅓, ⅔ or all of that Rank's body HP (Rank I: about 4 / 8 / 12). Brute and armored beasts may print a natural Defense bonus equal to the Rank bonus, the one Defense bonus a body ever prints, since gear is off. Natural weapons use the Form's own STR or DEX + the Rank bonus, with Rank-scaled dice: the body supplies the strength, so a WIL druid needs no STR, and WIL buys more shifts. A shift is a **fresh tally card** (Form body + Calling's INT and WIL + Aspects): only Aspects carry over, so a Form is a second build, and losing gear, Hands and voice is the brake on multiclassing. The separate HP pool is the second-body fantasy, and reaching 0 is the only way to lose a Form against your will (no condition ends it). The 1-hour cap keeps scouting Forms from replacing the body. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **The Rank bonus is hidden.** Players never see the term: cards print their numbers directly (weapon *+3 to hit*, book *Spell attack: INT + 3*, Knack *+3*, *Noisy 3*). Use the table's Bonus column to size every printed bonus or penalty.
- **Modifier boosts print twice.** A card that raises an ability modifier also prints the same number in the matching Defense row of its strip (+1 STR and +1 STR Defense), because Defense = 10 + modifier + bonuses. Every strip row then sums on its own.
- **Damage dice scale with Rank.** Cantrips and other growing effects print dice already scaled for their card's Rank:

| Card Rank | I–II | III | IV–V |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Damage dice** | ×1 | ×2 | ×3 |

  *Fire Bolt (1d10) in a Rank III book prints 2d10.*
- **Cards never grant dice.** Advantage and disadvantage come only from the rules: the three questions (P 5.2) and Subdue's decay (P 8). A card gives its Rank bonus as a flat bonus or penalty, or **changes a situation**: it creates one (a hit knocks Prone or Restrains, a step makes you unseen), changes a question's answer (*you count as two creatures for Crowd*, *long range gives you no disadvantage*, *Target: also when it's Frightened*) or protects (*Blur: attackers can't see you*). Dice then always come from the board, so a build never replaces positioning: Slots buy numbers, tactics buy dice. Rank IV–V cards bend a question; that is their game-breaking edge. Monsters and defensive cards answer the same way (a protective card that makes attackers unable to see you), so situations pull both ways.
- **Foe Defenses keep pace with Rank.** Foes are built from the same cards, so their Defenses grow with Rank like the Bearers' attacks (about +5 at Rank I to +12–13 at the peak). Aim a fair foe's main Defense at the party's usual attack bonus + 9 to 11 (50–60% base hit chance), where a die is worth most (+21 to +25 points between 30% and 70%). Off-Defenses don't grow (only the Calling's two +2s, Aspect modifiers and armor push Defenses up): from Rank III, give a foe no Defense below 10 + its Rank bonus. *Provisional: check when Rank III cards exist.*
- **Check bonuses** cover a **narrow activity** (sneaking, climbing, lying), never a whole ability, and equal the card's Rank bonus (*Cat Burglar*, Rank I: +2).
- **Light, precise weapons** (dagger, short blade, finesse blade, whip) deal one die size less than their heavier equivalents; their symbols lean DEX instead. Light blades keep the Rank's to-hit (+2) and get **Flurry** (+2 on later attacks with the same blade on your turn) as their edge.
- **Weapon keywords sized by expected damage** (+2 ability, Defense 13, `tools/balance_report.py`): *Greatsword* 2d6 5.75 per attack; *Greataxe* 1d12 5.42, with **Brutal** (crit: dice three times) 5.75; *Shortsword* 1d6 3.47 on the first attack, 4.02 with Flurry; *Dagger* 2.82 / 3.27. **Crushing** (*Greatclub*: Prone on a hit by 5 or more, about 1 hit in 3 against a fair Defense) is its edge over the *Glaive*'s reach; the Ogres carry it and are deliberately harder for it.
- **Magic armor, shields and weapons** are just higher-Rank cards with their own name with a bigger bonus and often a special effect (*Dragonscale*, Rank III, +5 STR Defense).

## 4. Designing Cards and Callings
**Callings and symbols** (replaced Cores; see CHANGELOG):
- **Twelve Callings**, two per pair of abilities: +2 / +2, +0 elsewhere (total +4, like the old starting Cores), Medium, HP 9–14 and Speed 30–35, one perk sized like a Rank I card. STR leans to HP, no STR is fragile; the two Callings of a pair never share both HP and Speed (in four pairs one has more HP, the other 5 ft more Speed: Sage and Tactician move 35 ft), and their perks pull different ways (Duelist moves, Brawler grabs; Templar guards, Zealot strikes; Spellblade casts, Tactician commands; Infiltrator sneaks, Ranger shoots; Performer helps, Trickster dodges; Sage feeds books, Hermit feeds WIL decks). A Calling has no Rank and never grows. *Provisional: HP, Speed and perks are tuned in the sim.*
- **Gating is loose on purpose:** a card is equipped if the Calling has **any one** of its lit symbols. A 2-symbol card fits 5 of 6 Callings (only the opposite pair is locked out), a 1-symbol card 3 of 6, an all-four card everyone. With the base set, every Calling can equip 13–14 of the 14 starter Hand cards (basic weapons light all four) and 13–17 of the 19 starter Aspects. The identity comes from the base +2s, the perk and the **one-symbol cards** (decks, heavy gear). **The lever:** to sharpen identity, give more cards a single symbol; to loosen it, more cards all four.
- **Symbol parity (check with `python3 tools/balance_report.py`).** A pair is locked out of its opposite pair's two-symbol cards and of the single-symbol cards of its two missing abilities. Equal access therefore needs **equal two-symbol counts per pair** and **equal single-symbol counts per ability**; the report counts what each pair can equip and actually use (a Focus needs a WIL deck, a Shield Technique a Shield). Base set: 59–69 usable cards of 79 (starter 35–41). Single-symbol identity cards: STR 5 (Plate, *Boarspear*, *Greatclub*, *Greataxe*, *Sweeping Blow*), WIL 3 (decks), INT 2 (*Conjurer's Book*, *Wand*), DEX 2 (*Shortbow*, *Fine Bow*); the starter heavy weapons light all four. Infiltrator and Ranger (DEX · INT) still see the least loot. *Open:* more DEX and INT identity cards (a second Spellbook, a DEX Technique) rather than taking STR's away.
- **Every lit symbol is a roll option.** A card that rolls names abilities from its own symbols, ideally all of them (*Battleaxe*, STR · DEX: *1d8 + STR or DEX*), so whoever can equip it rolls with a +2, never +0. Weapons attack with the higher of the abilities they name; basic weapons light more symbols than they roll (below). Decks print one symbol because their roll is one ability: Spellbooks INT, Divine, Glamour and Nature WIL. A card that triggers a general rule's roll (*Shield Bash* shoves with STR) is the one known gap: give such cards a symbol pair that includes the rule's ability.
- **Assigning symbols:** **A weapon's roll is STR, DEX or both**, never INT or WIL: heavy weapons STR alone, light and thrown weapons STR · DEX, precise blades and ranged weapons DEX alone, Versatile weapons STR or STR · DEX. **Staves and wands are the one exception:** they roll INT or WIL, one die size less than the matching weapon (*Spellguard Staff*, 1d6 / 1d8 Versatile; *Wand*, INT, 1d8 Lightning at range, against the *Fine Bow*'s 1d10), so an INT or WIL Bearer has a weapon that never outclasses a STR or DEX one. **Basic (starter) weapons light all four symbols** for compatibility: anyone can wield one, at +0 if the Calling lacks its roll. **Loot weapons light only their roll** (heavy ones STR alone, replacing the old Requirements). Heavy armor STR alone. This is the one place a card's symbols and its roll differ (see *Every lit symbol is a roll option* above). Aspects: two by theme, spread so each Calling gets a fair share of each Rank. Jewelry, Consumables, Recipes, Purses and Shards: all four (anyone).
- **HP lives on Armor and Traits** (section 3 table). Heavier armor gives more HP and less Speed; **every Trait prints HP** by Rank (Traits are what you *are*); Techniques and Knacks print none. Peak check: Templar 14 + Rank IV heavy armor 30 + four Rank IV Traits 40 = 84 HP (the old Rank IV Core: 80–100). Fragile end: a Sage in light armor with two Traits, 9 + 10 + 20 = 39. Start: Templar in Mail with two Traits 22, Sage in Leather 11 (old starting Cores 9–14). *Open: is the spread too wide? Sim pass.*
- **Rank V can rewrite a Calling:** a Rank V Aspect that, when absorbed, changes the Calling for good and is used up. Cards that no longer match stop working until swapped out. Name and card TBD (none in the base set).
- **Foes have no Calling:** they use any card. A foe's body is plain text; its HP leaves out the HP its Armor and Traits print. When Cores were removed, every base-set foe kept its exact totals (body = old Core, minus card HP), so the sim measured only the player-side change.
- **Species are Traits or Knacks** (Traits *Human Grit*, *Elven Blood*, *Dwarven Blood*, *Halfling Luck*: a body edge; Knacks *Orcish Blood*, *Hobgoblin Discipline*: an upbringing, four activities and no HP, so they never beat a plain Knack): Rank I loot within the Trait budget (GM 4.3): +2 HP (Traits) and one edge each (darkvision, a narrow Defense bonus, a once-per-rest reroll or +2 after a failed check, or four activities). Every species card is fixed: what you see is what you get, with no choice made when you gain it. Humanoid foes keep their darkvision as plain-text senses. A Bearer's species is flavor; a species Trait is a loot choice, and humanoid foes drop them.

- **Uses sit on the power they limit**, as a tag on its activation (*Reaction (1/S.R.)*) or opening it (*2/S.R.*), never in the keywords, so a reader never wonders whether they cover the whole card. Whole-card pools (spell points, Resolve, Recipe flasks) stay where they are.
- **Every card is unique.** A name never carries its Rank: a stronger card gets a new name, not a numeral.
- Keep each card to **one clear idea** that fits on a card. The card says what it does: anything that overrides a rule (swap time, underwater use, size) is printed on it.
- **Size every number by Rank.** All bonuses stack, so a card's bonus or penalty equals its **Rank bonus**, and a card that attacks on its own uses a **choice of two abilities + its Rank bonus** (*Alchemist's Fire Recipe*, Rank II: DEX or INT + 3). Pick two abilities that serve different Callings; on a gated card they are its symbols. Spellbooks keep INT alone.
- **Trait budget (GM 4.3).** For Traits and Knacks, **Rank = budget** (I 1 … V 5). A bonus (+1 modifier) costs 1, a perk 1, a strong perk 2, an activity ¼ (at most 4 per card, from at least two abilities). Never a lone bonus. Rank I has no bonus or perk (activities or one narrow edge). Families: Cross, Dual, Gifted (from Rank II). Concept cards in GM 4.4, random generator in GM 4.5. Techniques, weapons, Jewelry and other types are sized by the Rank table, not by the budget. *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **Callings, not classes: encourage multiclassing.** Most cards print two symbols, so most loot fits most Callings. About **half** of all cards don't roll (flat bonuses, reactions, rerolls, movement, senses, uses); the other half (weapons, Spellbooks, Divine, Glamour and Nature, attack cards) roll the abilities of their symbols.
  - **Bridge cards** link two styles: a Technique that buffs allies when you hit (*Battle Hymn*), a weapon that lets you cast (*Spellguard Staff*). Put some in every loot table.
- Give DEX and WIL real value. DEX already has a weapon Defense, initiative and ranged attacks. WIL has its Defense against charm and fear but few combat uses, so it needs social cards with teeth (allies, discounts, rallying, demoralizing) to be worth investing in. Divine, Glamour and Nature (P 10.6) are WIL's magic: buffs, debuffs, reactions, healing and Forms. Only Forms deal damage. The base set starts players on Glamour only (*Wanderer's Repertoire*, *Dancer's Bells*): the cleric and druid roles (Litany, Remembrance, *Holy Amulet*) are loot, found in play. Social rewards are cards that take real Slots: favors (Consumable), contacts (Jewelry with uses), titles and renown (Knack).
- Give every card a type keyword that names its Slot (P 11.1). **Jewelry** holds worn magic (rings, amulets, cloaks, gloves, boots), usually active powers with limited uses; other passive bonuses belong in Aspects.
- **Never print the same thing twice.** Keywords stay whole in `cards/base_set.py` (the sim and the rules read them), but `tools/build_cards.py` (`printed`) leaves out what the card's text already says: *Range* and *Thrown*, whose range is in the attack or casting line, and a Versatile weapon's die, which is in its damage.
- **The look by family.** Every card type has a symbol, printed at the bottom left and faint behind the text. Under the name, the family in grey: Calling, Equipment, Item, Aspect, or Spell / Power on a deck card. The band marks the family: black for a Calling, a double rule under it for Equipment, hatched for an Item, the type symbol before the name for an Aspect; a deck card prints its cover's symbol at the foot of its index (hatched for a Spellbook). Keywords sit at the foot on two lines: type and rule keywords, then descriptive ones in grey; each line shrinks to fit. A Calling's footer leaves out its type, which the subtitle says. Type symbols are ink drawings in `rules/symbols/ink/` (made by `tools/gen_symbols.py` and `tools/ink_symbols.py`), with sketches in `rules/symbols/sketch/` until each is drawn.
- Armor adds mostly to STR Defense; Leather's +1 DEX Defense lets nimble characters trade 5 ft of speed for it or go unarmored to move faster.
- Card design should keep the **bookkeeping cost of a swap** low (how much math a swap triggers).
- **Never write on cards.** The only component written on is the **tally card** (P 12.3), which is disposable. Everything else that changes is shown with tokens (uses) or with cards (gold cards in a Purse). Spell points and Resolve are counted on the tally card.
- **Name the card where the concept lives.** Rules speak of gear, Aspects, Purses and bags of coins. Each concept that a card represents (Calling, gear, Aspect, Shard, Purse and gold, Spellbook, Litany, Repertoire and Remembrance decks, Pack and Stash piles) gets one short "At the table…" sentence where the rules define it, pointing to P 12. Elsewhere, "card" appears only in the table-setup chapter (P 12). The rules stay playable with a plain sheet. Slots are objects as **Slot frames** (P 12.2): a frame holds a card and is never one, so "card" still means a Calling, gear, an Aspect, a spell or gold.
- **Card layout (P 12.1):** name, symbols and Rank on top, bonus strip on the right, effect in the center, keywords at the bottom. The **symbol corner** is a 2 × 2 grid of the four ability symbols beside the Rank (STR DEX / INT WIL), the unused ones greyed out, so "can I equip this?" reads at a glance in a loot pile. The Calling card's strip holds only its own values. The **tally card** is the character sheet: card-sized, its strip lines up with the bonus strips and holds the ten totals; beside it, current HP (scratch box), temporary HP and death saves. Nothing derivable (attack bonuses, passive Awareness) goes on it. A card may have both a strip and an effect: players spread cards out to play and stack them (tally card first, then the Calling, each card on top one strip to the left, no vertical offset, so the rows line up) only to add up, so the strips read as a sum ending on the tally card. A swap is one subtraction and one addition.
- **Lore line.** Each card, if space permits, ends with a few sentences of lore at the bottom of its effect area, smaller and in italic. Lore never states or changes a rule, so dropping it loses nothing. It follows the lore constraints (1b): an Aspect is told as what someone was or knew (often the being it came from), a Calling as who someone is, gear as an object someone made and carried. Keep it setting-light (no place names, no fixed answer to GM 6.1), one or two short sentences, about 25 words. The build shrinks the art first, then drops the lore when the card still overflows. Source: `LORE` in `cards/base_set.py`. Foe cards carry no lore: their space goes to the build.
- **Tally card back: wishlist (TODO, with the card backs).** The back of the tally card holds 3 write-in lines for cards the player wants (GM 4.2). It tells the GM which drops will land without asking, and gives players a goal between stories. Not in PLAYERS.md or the card templates yet: add it when card backs are designed.
- **Slot frames (P 12.2).** Every Slot is a physical holder, so the build is a physical act and survives between sessions: **framed = equipped**, loose = Pack, Stash or ground. Frames make the Slot count visible (no "how many Aspect Slots do I have?"), and growth is a moment at the table: the GM hands over a new Aspect frame at each milestone and a unique frame (Third Hand, Second Jewel, Great Backpack) from a Boss. Extra Gear Slot frames never go into the Pack. Shards use the same frame, so capture, absorb and swap are the same gesture. Specs: **67 × 98 mm** for a 63 × 88 mm card, home-printed on unsleeved cardstock, 8 per landscape Letter page; top band of 8 mm (Slot symbol, name, swap time), 2.5 mm left border, 1.5 mm right border so the card's strip sits on the frame's edge and framed cards stack to add up (P 12.3); **two photo-corner slits**, top right (cut into the band) and bottom left, opposite corners so the card can't slide out in storage; the center, hidden under the card, prints what an empty Slot does (the Armor frame prints +10 Speed in its strip row). Decks frame only their cover; a Consumable stack shares one frame. Source: `cards/base_set.py`, built by `tools/build_cards.py`.
- **Special Slots (future, not in the rules).** A special Slot is a frame of the same type, found as loot with a Rank, that **replaces** one of your plain frames. Its bonus belongs to the **Slot**, not the card, so it boosts whatever card sits in it and stays with your body when you change gear (*a Hand frame that gives +2 Attack to its weapon*). It prints its bonus in the top band and in the center. Open: whether special frames can be traded or lost, how strong they get by Rank, and whether the bonus may go in a strip (the frame's right border would have to widen into a second strip).
- **One word, one meaning.** The vocabulary lives in `GLOSSARY.md` (repo root): each term has one meaning, and its _Avoid_ words are never used for it. The handbooks' Glossary chapters are generated from it (`tools/sync_glossary.py`, run by the PDF build); edit the glossary there, never in the handbooks.
- **Keywords carry the rules.** When several cards share a mechanic, make it a keyword instead of repeating text, and add it to P Chapter 11. Never reuse a reserved word with another meaning. Keyword order: type, properties, uses.
- **Conditional bonuses** (*+2 STR Defense against poison*) go in the effect text, never in the strip: the strip holds only what always applies.
- **Spellbook decks (P 12.4):** the cover card sits in front of the fan; spell cards fan behind it. Each spell card has a **left-edge index** that stays visible in a hand fan or a sideways spread: spell point cost at the top corner, casting-time and target-Defense icons, the name running down the edge, and the book's name. Order spells by cost. Keep a deck small enough to fan in one hand (cover + 6 to 10 spells). Litanies, Repertoires and Remembrances follow the same layout (Resolve cost at the corner); a Remembrance holds 2–4 Forms by Rank.
- **Foe cards (GM 2.1).** A monster is built from player cards: its card prints a plain-text body (modifiers, HP, Speed, size) and names its gear and Aspects in *italics*, by name and **card ID**, and the build adds up totals and attacks (`cards/foes.py`, built by `tools/build_cards.py` into the base set with blank foe cards). Write only what no card could be as **plain text** (anatomy, a curse, giant strength): it never drops, and it may change how the monster's italic cards perform (extra dice, extra HP) while the card that drops stays the normal one. A foe card **includes its body**: another body is another foe card. Bosses carry the **Boss** tag, group leaders the **Leader** keyword (Morale, P 6.11). Foe cards are **double width** (126 × 88 mm, 3 per Letter page): the left half is a drawing (`card_art.FOE_ART`), the right half a normal card with its strip on the right edge, so a tucked card's strip still lines up. The drop line names each card in full with its ID. A foe may use a few powers of a deck it carries (`powers`); the whole deck drops. Card text on a foe card prints the foe's numbers (*WIL + 2* → *+5*). Keep plain text rare: every power a player watches being used against them should be a card they could win.
- **Classic monsters (GM 2.2).** Built like the rest: italic cards that drop, plain text for anatomy. *Undead* (plain text): resists Poison and Necrotic, vulnerable to Radiant, never checks Morale. *Relentless* (Trait, Rank II) is the card behind "won't stay down" (Orc, Zombie). The Troll's *Regeneration* and claws are plain text; at 80 HP and two 2d6 + 4 claws it sits beside the Ogre as a brute. *Provisional: tune the Troll and the Zombie in the sim.*
- **Card IDs (P 12.1):** type letter, Rank, number in order of appearance in `cards/base_set.py` (*W-II-02*). Letters: L Calling (no Rank: *L-01*) · A Armor · W Weapon · S Shield · M Instrument · J Jewelry · P Consumable · R Recipe · U Purse · B Spellbook · T Trait · Q Technique · K Knack · D Divine · G Glamour · N Nature. Add new cards **at the end** of their type and Rank so existing IDs never change.

## 5. Design Notes
- **Shards are the Aspect economy.** Aspects have no body of their own, so every Aspect kept costs an empty Shard, a Pack or Stash card, and (when looted) 1 action within a minute of the kill. This turns loot choice into a real decision at the body, not in the Stash weeks later, and keeps the Stash from filling with ordinary drops. Numbers: 3 carried Shards per character (bulky, raised by Jewelry so it competes with active jewels), 2 per character in the Stash, 250 gp each (a Rank I card costs 100 gp: an empty Shard is worth more than an ordinary Aspect, so players release junk instead of hoarding it). Pack 8 = 3 Shards + the old 5 gear cards; Stash 5 per character (upgrades 8/12/20, the old 10/15/25/40 ratios) so it scales with party size. Release is free and swaps need no extra Shard, so a long-rest rebuild never stalls on Shard count. Capture skips the Pack cost so it stays a 1-action choice in combat (finish an enemy or save its Aspect).
- **Gold is gear.** A Purse holds gold up to a Rank cap (100 / 500 / 2,000 / 10,000 / no limit) and costs 1/10 of its capacity (10 / 50 / 200 / 1,000 / 5,000 gp); its gold takes no room, loose bags of coins take 1 piece each. Paying needs the Purse nearby, so the party chooses who carries the money and where it sits (Item Slot, Pack, Stash), with exposure to theft and disaster left to the GM. The starting *Coin Pouch* (Rank I) takes 1 of the 8 Pack pieces. Gold cards (1, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1,000 gp) keep the amount on the table without writing.
- **One engine, one Slot.** Every build's engine costs one Slot: a weapon a Hand, a Spellbook an Item (plus an empty Hand to cast), a Divine, Glamour or Nature Aspect an Aspect Slot. The WIL engine costs a growing Slot (1 of 3 at the start, 1 of 8 at the peak) but frees a Hand and an Item. Aspect Slots are the one shared growth track, so parity comes from loot: every style gets an equal share of Aspects at each Rank (GM 4.1). *Playtest: if WIL builds lag at 3 Aspect Slots, revisit (a dedicated magic Slot or a 4th starting Aspect Slot were the alternatives).*
- **WIL magic is an Aspect, INT magic is gear.** A Spellbook is a book you hold; prayer, art and the memory of beasts are what you are. So WIL magic swaps only at a long rest and lives in Shards; Instruments and other **Focus** gear are the fast-swap, stealable part.
- **Swap times.** Fast Slots (Hand, Jewelry, Item) keep fights tactical; slow Slots (Armor, Aspect) fix the build between rests. The Pack cost (action + all movement) makes mid-fight swaps a real trade-off; the expended lock stops cycling spent jewels between fights (spent spell points stay on the tally card whatever book you hold).
- **Subdue replaces Legendary Resistance and saves (P 8).** The target loses its next turn; then the source repeats the attack (full bonus, so stacking helps it last too) with decay: one disadvantage die per repeat that hit and per time the source took damage, kept by a die beside the target. Design target: a Subdue lasts 2–3 turns, rarely 4 (under 20%). In the sim (`sim/dev/subdue.js`, a Rank I Stunning spell on the toughest foe, 7 QA fights, 40 seeds, 3d20 cap): regular foes lost 1 turn 53%, 2 turns 38%, 3 turns 9%, 4+ 0% (avg 1.6), and two-thirds of them died while held (so real holds run longer than that); a Boss lost avg 2 turn-or-half-turns (about one round), 4+ 10%. The win rate didn't move (55–59%). The old rule (bare-modifier maintain rolls, dice per hit on the source and the target, weakening) lasted 1 turn 57% of the time and needed three counters. Spending both actions to hold on (skip a decay step) barely mattered in the sim (+0.2 turns): kept as a choice for a caster with nothing better to do. A non-Subdue impairing effect makes one attack roll and lasts what it prints (2 turns for Web's Restrained and Dread's Frightened, 1 minute for Bane): no maintain rolls at all.
- **High spells cost more instead of once a day (P 10.2).** 5th level costs 6 points and 6th costs 8: the pool is the brake, with no per-book flag to track. A Rank III book (+9, max 5th) with INT +4 gives 13 points: one 5th-level spell and 7 left; a Rank V book (+18) with INT +5 gives 23: two 6th-level spells, plus 7.
- **One pool each, on the tally card (P 10.2, 10.6, 12.3).** Spell points = every equipped book + INT (minimum 1); Resolve = every Divine, Glamour and Nature deck + WIL once (minimum 1). Extra points from Callings, Traits and gear add to the total, with no pool to choose. The tally card's box reads *total – marks*: write the total, draw one mark per point spent, erase at the rest. This replaces tokens on deck covers and per-deck pools: one number to track, WIL counted once (two decks no longer double it), and INT now buys spell points the way WIL buys Resolve. Book pools dropped by 2 (the starting INT) at every Rank to compensate. A book or deck can come off while the points spent still fit in the new total, so the expended lock now covers uses only. In the sim (`sim/dev/batch.js --seeds 100 --by`) the 6 QA fights come out identical: the sample party's pools don't move (Isolde 4 + 1 → 2 + INT 2 + 1 = 5; Aldo has one deck), so the change shows only with INT above +2, two books or two WIL decks (the foe Mage goes from 4 to 5). *Provisional until the balance pass.*
- **No costly components.** Powerful spells are gated by the Spellbook's Rank and by loot instead of gold.
- **No concentration**, for snappiness, and no Lasting effects: only Subdue repeats its roll. Without a one-spell limit, buffs stack; keep their durations short (1 minute = 10 rounds) when converting, and watch stacked blessings in playtests.
- **Trials, not fights, are the unit of play.** The play loop (P 1), the loot budget (GM 4.1) and the milestone pace all count trials, and a fight is one kind. A talked-out or snuck-past trial pays the same as a won fight, so no player is pushed into combat to get loot, and quiet builds (WIL, INT, DEX) earn as much as attack builds. The XP budget (GM 5) sizes fights only, since non-combat trials are sized by DC and reward.
- **Story → Quest → Trial.** Each scale has its own payoff: cards per trial, an optional quest reward per quest, a milestone per story. Milestones are party-wide only (no personal goals), so the loop always pulls the party forward together and no character's Slot count pulls ahead. The loop is named "play loop", not "core loop", to keep the old Core term out, and its unit is a "trial" because Challenge Rating already uses "challenge". Exploration lives at the quest scale, not in the loop: it has no mechanism of its own, and every loop step is a moment of play (a roll, a loot choice, a build change).
- **Everything stacks, and dice limit themselves.** Each extra d20 is worth less than the one before. Flat-bonus equivalent of each die at a 50% base: +5.0, +2.5, +1.3 (25% base: +3.8, +2.8, +2.1). Totals: 2d20 ≈ +5, 3d20 ≈ +7.5, 4d20 ≈ +9, so dice never run away the way flat bonuses can. Kept-die spread shrinks (SD 5.8 → 4.7 → 3.9 → 3.3), so under advantage each flat +1 is worth up to 7–8% instead of 5%. The problem is never the stacking but cheap, always-on sources: control where dice come from, not how far they stack. Raising every bonus or Defense to compensate was rejected: unadvantaged attacks fall to 20–30%, every turn becomes a hunt for dice, maintain rolls (bare modifier) fall behind, and the numbers grow.
- **Crits scale with dice.** Chance of a natural 20 on the kept die: 5% · 9.8% · 14.3% · 18.5% (1–4d20). Price "on a critical hit" effects for stacked attacks.
- **Outnumbered compares sides, around the target only.** One die to the side with more creatures around the target: the attacker's side engaged with the target (attacker included) against the target and its allies within 5 ft of it. Counting the target's allies "engaged with your side" anywhere on the map was a chained lookup; now the GM looks at one spot, and a second orc helps only by standing beside the first, so holding a line means standing shoulder to shoulder. Not one die per creature: counting the difference on every attack was the slowest step at the table, and the yes/no comparison is a glance. Everyone attacking the same target gets the same dice, so a back-line archer or caster gains nothing over the melee (counting only the attacker's allies would give a non-engaged attacker one extra die). A lone attacker at range against an unengaged target: 0 against 0, no dice. Rewards holding a line and fighting in pairs; a bigger crowd adds nothing more.
- **Monsters share the players' cards.** The party sees a power used against it, then can win the very card: enemies with strong flavor become a shopping list, and the GM never converts loot. Building a monster is choosing cards, so a custom foe takes minutes (tuck a loot card behind a foe card, or fill a blank one). The foe card never leaves the GM; drops are copies pulled from the loot pool by ID, so a monster can be replayed forever. Foe cards stay hidden: what a monster carried is learned when it falls. Uses reset each fight so nothing is tracked between trials. "Monstrous" was removed: plain text on the foe card does its job without a keyword, and only italic cards drop, so six legionaries don't drop six pila (the trial's loot budget picks).
- **Open: foe difficulty.** How many cards and which Ranks make a fair foe, and whether CR and XP should be computed from them (like the Trait budget), is undecided. For now foe cards print no CR or XP (keywords instead: Foe, Boss, size, senses); the GM judges CR (GM 5). No roles (minion, elite): rejected for now.
- **Wounds replace Exhaustion (P 7.6).** A Wound at every fall to 0 HP makes HP matter beyond the fight: the death-save loop stays, but going down now costs something that lasts. Only downtime (3 days in a safe place) heals them, so the party's distance from safety becomes a resource the GM controls, and quests gain a natural return trip. No maximum: the penalty grows until a Bearer is no longer worth fielding, which pushes retreat and care, not death. The deck (20 cards, shared, no duplicates while drawn): 12 light penalties (one number or one roll type), 5 Crippling (a Hand, Jewelry or Item Slot; never Aspect or Armor, which would force losing an Aspect without a Shard or putting Armor in the Pack), 3 grave (two penalties, or −10 HP). Penalties print in the strip when they hit a total, so they stack like gear. Hazards (cold, hunger, thirst) give Wounds; drowning takes you to 0 HP. Healing magic never touches Wounds: only downtime and rare remedies (*Restorative Salve*, Rank II Consumable; later a Rank IV Mend). Monsters never take Wounds.
- **Outnumbered vs area attacks.** Outnumbered (P 6.5) rewards ganging up on one enemy. Counter it by giving most monsters at least one area attack (a sweep, a burst, a breath) so clustering around a single target has a cost. To be made explicit later in a "Building monsters and balanced fights" section of the GM's Guide.
- **Information design: public rules, partly hidden state, announced intentions.** Every rule a foe uses is public: its keywords (Boss, Leader, Recharge 5–6) are in P 11.6 and Morale is a player rule (P 6.11), so players can plan around them. The state is partly hidden: foe cards stay with the GM, and what a monster carries is learned when it falls, by spying (GM 2.3: one line, the strip, or the whole card on a big success, one try per spy and group, a failed hide costs an ambush or a cut-off spy) or by watching it act. Intentions are announced: a recharged power is described when it comes back (GM 2.1), so a big hit is a threat the party sees coming and can answer (spread out, Dodge, kill it first), never a surprise from nowhere.
- **Recharge rolls at the end of the monster's turn.** Same odds as at the start, but the roll now lands before the Bearers act, so the GM can telegraph the ready power (the ogre draws back its club) and the players get a side-turn to react. At the start of its turn it would fire the moment it came back. A Boss's half turn doesn't roll, so the half turn can't double the recharge rate.
- **Morale makes target priority matter.** In the bandit sims (`build/bandits/REPORT.md`, F5), killing the Bandit Captain first was no better than killing the nearest bandit: a leader's death removed only its own damage. With Morale (WIL check DC 10 when the Leader falls and at half the group down; now one roll for the group with its best WIL, so the GM rolls once and the fight usually ends or goes on whole), Captain-first went from mid-table to the top two (+25–35 win points), most sound plans gained 5–15 points, fights got ~1 round shorter, and bad plans (splitting up, chasing archers) stayed bad. A Boss never checks, so Boss fights keep their length. Side effect: fights get easier; size Leader groups about one step harder (to add to GM 5). Fleeing or surrendering is the GM's call, so a fled foe can return, bargain or be questioned.
- **Downed creatures don't engage (P 6.5).** An Incapacitated creature is engaged with no one either way, so foes next to a downed Bearer stop counting for Outnumbered and its allies aren't shot at 4d20 keep lowest. A clarification, worth +2–4 win points to everyone in the sims.
- **Initiative is simple, and a sneak always pays off (P 6.1).** A side that sneaks up unnoticed goes first with full turns, no roll: the party's effort at stealth (or the GM's foes sneaking past a careless party) can't be undone by a bad die. Surprise no longer rolls with disadvantage. Otherwise each side adds its **best DEX**: one number to look up, no averaging or rounding, and the scout's DEX matters. Going first isn't automatically good under the first-mover rule, so a high-DEX party gains little in an open fight; the payoff is in sneaking. All-Invisible sides keep their advantage die on the roll (P 8).
- **Three questions (P 5.2).** Situational dice were one per source, Outnumbered one per creature, which meant counting before every attack. Now You, Target and Crowd each give one die at most, so situations reach 3 dice at most (4d20). In the sim (`sim/dev/batch.js`, the 7 QA fights, 100 seeds, same policy) the change is close to neutral and slightly harder on the party: PC wins Road 83 → 80, Wolves 100 → 100, Ogre 95 → 97, Chieftain alone 83 → 79, Sample Boss 28 → 22, Goblins 17 → 17, Legionaries 26 → 26. The policy rarely stacked more than one die per question, so the change is mostly about table speed, not balance.

- **Advantage on damage (P 5.2).** Once you'd hit anyway, extra attack dice are wasted; moving them to damage gives them a use, adds a choice to advantaged attacks, and lets damage builds lean on advantage. Cancel first, then split; choose before rolling; disadvantage always stays on the attack (else every penalty softens); the moved die rerolls the whole damage, crit dice included; area damage rerolls once for every target; only dice move, never flat bonuses. Break-even (expected damage, crits ignored; attack dice also raise crits): a die on damage pays once the single-d20 hit chance reaches about 80–85%, i.e. hitting on a natural 4–5: 1d12+3 79%, 1d8+3 83%, 1d6+3 85%, 2d10+4 85%, 3d10+5 87% (the flat part grows with Rank, so the threshold creeps up). Hence the table rule "worth it when you'd hit on a 5 or better". In the sim (`sim/dev/batch.js`, 100 seeds, both sides split for the best expected damage) 14% of leftover dice moved and every fight stayed within ±1 win of keeping them on the attack: at Rank I, hit chances rarely reach 80%. A "move at 75%" policy moved 32% and cost the party (Chieftain alone 90 → 84 wins). Removing the 3d20 cap changed no fight result; Subdue holds stayed about 1.5 turns (regular) and 2 (Boss). Expect the option to matter at higher Ranks and against off-Defenses.
- **No stacking cap.** Limited Slots are the limit for bonuses: a card spent on one number isn't spent elsewhere. Dice need no cap: only the three questions give them, so an attack rolls 4d20 at most; only decay grows past that, and it ends the effect. Offense saturates (a natural 1 caps a single d20 at 95%, so stacking to-hit beyond ~90% is wasted). Advantage dice break that cap: a natural 1 counts only on the kept die, so 3d20 against an easy Defense hits about 99%, and advantage on damage (P 5.2) gives those dice a use. Lopsided defenders are answered by the GM choosing another angle: a STR/DEX stacker gets attacked on INT or WIL, or with area effects; any stacker has other Defenses. Fight-ending spells are limited by Subdue's decay.
- **Two actions and a reaction (P 6.2).** Movement is an action (Move, split around the other action), so Dash is gone, and there is no bonus action. Every choice is one currency: moving costs an attack, so positioning, range and hit-and-run carry real weight. Attack and Cast can each be taken twice (no limit on spells per turn). Old bonus actions became an **action** when they heal, shift or buff the group (potions, *Second Wind*, *Healing Word*, Anthems, Forms) and a **reaction** otherwise (flasks, *Steady Aim*, *Shield Bash*, *Misty Step*, *Slippery*, Bellow): a quick trick on your turn costs your opportunity attack or your defensive reaction until your next turn. Two-weapon fighting is a reaction. *Twin Strike* adds one attack once per turn (3 at most). Disengage costs an action and is impossible when surrounded (3+ enemies engaged with you): retreating from even one foe costs an attack, so hit-and-run is a real trade, and surrounded matches the Crowd die (3 against 1). Standing up takes an action. The first-mover rule and the Boss half turn are one action.
- **What the switch did in the sim** (`sim/dev/batch.js`, the 7 QA fights, 40 seeds, same policy): fights got 25–40% shorter, and Bearer damage per round rose 30–65% (not ×2: whoever moves loses an attack). Standing still pays most: casters (two Magic Missiles a turn), archers and slingers (Goblins +65%: 2 vs 6 Goblins fell from 15 to 8 wins out of 40). Lone big foes fell behind: the Chieftain alone went from 20 to 29 party wins out of 40, the Ogre dies in about 3 rounds, because four Bearers attacking twice each multiply Outnumbered. Heavy weapons gain the most standing still (Greatsword 6.8 → 13.7 expected per turn at Defense 13); a Light pair falls from 93% to 77% of a Greatsword and now spends the reaction. Loading loses most of its cost (one shot per action).
- **What the pillar pass did in the sim** (3d20 cap, local Outnumbered, group Morale, Disengage, Subdue; `sim/dev/batch.js`, 40 seeds, same seeds before and after): PC wins Road 31 → 33, Wolves 40 → 40, Ogre 40 → 40, Chieftain alone 31 → 35, Sample Boss 11 → 8, Goblins 8 → 4, Legionaries 7 → 17. The big mover is the legion: its three Legionaries stand 10 ft apart, so under the local count they no longer back each other up, and one failed Morale roll now breaks the whole group. Foes that should fight as a line must stand shoulder to shoulder (add to GM 5). Two Bearers against six Goblins got harder: the goblins crowd one target while the pair can't help each other unless they stand side by side.
- **What Callings did in the sim** (`sim/dev/batch.js --seeds 40 --by`, same seeds, before = Cores at HEAD): the sample party became Templar (Bram), Infiltrator (Wren), Sage (Isolde), Performer (Aldo) with the same cards; foes kept their exact totals. PC wins: Road 33 → 37, Wolves 40 → 40, Ogre 40 → 40, Chieftain alone 34 → 36, Sample Boss 9 → 12, Goblins (2 vs 6) 4 → 1, Legionaries 20 → 27. PC deaths over all fights 53 → 17: the HP on Armor and Traits (Bram 14 → 22) keeps Bearers up. Damage moved to the Sage (staff and book on INT, +1 spell point: Ogre 14.6 → 17.9 DPR); the Templar and Infiltrator lost a point of their main ability (+2 instead of the Brute's and Nimble's +3), which sinks the two-Bearer Goblin fight (Wren 5.7 → 3.1 DPR). *Open:* starting HP is now a bit high for the old encounter budget, and +2 / +2 weakens a one-ability specialist; options are a Calling at +3 / +1 (the player picks) or +1 to-hit on Rank I weapons. Oath and Crowd Work are used whenever the policy can.
- **Open after the switch:** raise Boss and brute HP (about +30–50%), watch the casters' two-spell nova over a whole adventuring day (single fights don't show it), give Light weapons an edge of their own (done: Flurry), and give maps more cover against stationary shooters. Retest with `node sim/dev/batch.js --by`.
